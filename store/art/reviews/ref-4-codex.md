Pick: 2b.

The sharp video gives the blurred lock a recognizable source. The buyer sees the viewing state and the interruption in the same frame. That matters more than showing the counter at exactly zero.

The original has the largest, most readable lock, but its surrounding color fields do little explanatory work. Without the caption, it could be a standalone break reminder.

2a has the clearest explicit cause-and-effect diagram. Its weakness is that the zero-minute counter takes substantial space without showing the video being interrupted. It also changes the set’s composition more sharply.

2b’s weakness is sequence. Two stacked players can briefly look like two separate examples. Add small frame labels above the captures: “Before” and “When time runs out.” These are caption labels, outside the real interface. This is useful polish, not a shipping requirement.

2b belongs beside the other four frames. It keeps the cream caption panel, dark capture area, typography, and leader line. The different arrangement inside the capture area is acceptable.

The 480px and 560px panel widths are not a real problem. The seam moves, but the branding and hierarchy remain stable. Keeping the important interface text readable matters more than fixing that seam. Keep the mixed widths. I would not move every frame to 480px.
