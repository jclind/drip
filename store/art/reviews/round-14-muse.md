Image 1

What pushes the scores: The headline and single-video crop state the idea immediately. The legend explains drops and cards before the UI has to. Against that: the left panel is half empty air between subline and legend, the leader line is a long hairline crossing the frame seam to a small dot, and the sample state reads wrong on its own numbers, 34m left next to 2 drops at 15m each. Design loses points for the dead middle and for how small the actual pick sits in the frame. AI look is driven mainly by the thumbnail illustration, generic flat mountains, blobby storm cloud, gradient sky, tiny cyclist with no specific detail, and by the templated beige panel plus thin blue diagram line.

What works: One video, one Watch button, tokens explained in plain words.

What to fix, ordered by impact:
1. Illustration: redraw with specific, imperfect detail, a particular trail, gear, weather, and a cyclist with weight and scale. Kill the smooth gradient sky and the symmetrical cloud blob. This is the single biggest AI-look problem in the set.
2. Sample data: make it 30m left today with 2 drops, or show the leftover minutes separately. As shown, the arithmetic contradicts the legend two inches away.
3. Crop and scale: enlarge the right strip about 10 to 15 percent and trim the caption panel's empty middle by moving the legend and note block up as one group. Do not move the legend alone.
4. Caption copy: shorten the subline to one line at thumbnail weight, "Replaces YouTube's home feed with one pick." The Chrome extension fact belongs on the tile and listing text, not taking two lines here.
5. Leader line: thicken it slightly, shorten it by widening the right strip, and land the dot flush on the Reveal next pill edge. At small size it currently vanishes, then reappears as a stray dot.

To reach 9: tighter crop, no dead middle, correct sample arithmetic, illustration with real specificity. To reach 2: redrawn thumbnail, a leader that looks placed by hand rather than generated as an annotation layer, and type spacing with less even, template-like regularity.

The set

Does the order tell the story in the first one or two images: Yes, mostly. One video at a time, then time running out, is the product in two frames, and putting the limit second is braver and better than leading with settings. The problem is that frame 1 asks the viewer to learn two currencies and frame 2 introduces a third, unlocks, before any frame has said why one video is better than the feed. Mode choice at 3 is the natural third beat. History at 5 as the closer is weak, it is evidence, not a reason, and it ends the carousel on the least emotional frame. Swapping 4 and 5 would end on the trust argument, easy to tighten, slow to loosen, which is the more memorable last impression.

Consistency across the five frames: Left panel width, headline serif, subline grey, and the small drip mark are consistent and hold the set together. Three things break it. Frame 2's dark water band is the only coloured block on a caption panel, so its left side is a different height and weight from the other four. Frame 5's right strip is charcoal where 1, 3, and 4 are black. Note placement wanders, sometimes two notes, sometimes one, at unrelated heights, with leader lengths varying from two inches to half the frame. Caption length also varies, frame 4's subline is two stacked one-sentence lines, frame 1's wraps long, frame 5's has a third microtype line. Panel fill is the systemic issue: every caption panel is 40 to 55 percent empty, and the emptiness sits in different places, middle in 1 and 2, lower half in 3 and 5. A viewer flipping the carousel feels the layout shift each time.

Legibility at small thumbnail size: Headlines survive. Nothing else reliably does. Subline, notes, leader lines, channel name, view counts, settings rows, and chart labels all fall below readable size, and frame 4 becomes a dark rectangle with blue toggles. The tile is the only image fully legible small. Since the store shows these small in search and category surfaces, each screenshot currently communicates one serif sentence and a dark blur at that size. Enlarging every right strip and cutting one idea per frame is worth more than any copy polish.

Copy flagged as AI-written or generic: "Time for a breather." "Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen." The balanced antithesis construction is the most recognisable AI copywriting rhythm in the set, and it appears twice. "Water rises as your watch time runs out" is generic and restates its own headline. "Your week in minutes" is a fragment with no verb. "Choose how strict" is a fragment missing its object. The concrete lines are the good ones and none of them are symmetrical: "Not this one? The next pick costs a card." "41 minutes a day, down from 48 the week before." More of the second kind, fewer of the first.

Rejection and misleading risks: Three, in order of seriousness. First, frame 1 presents an invented video with an invented channel, "Wet Gravel Club, 84K views, 1w ago," with no sample label on that frame. Frame 5 labels its sample data and frame 1 does not, and the drawn thumbnail does not by itself tell a store reviewer this is not a real YouTube video with real view counts. That is fabricated social proof on its face and the most likely rejection or misleading-content flag. Label it as an illustration or strip the view count from the sample data. Second, the internal arithmetic contradictions, 34m against 2 drops of 15m, and 1h watched with a drop still for sale. Store reviewers and users both read these as the product not working as described. Third, and minor, naming YouTube in the subline and tagline context is nominative and fine, and no YouTube logo or red play button styling is used, the Watch pill is black and white, so branding risk is low. Keep it that way. The "15% from the week before" and "down from 48" figures are labelled sample data on frame 5, which covers them, provided that label gets bigger as noted above.

Ranked top 5 fixes across the whole set:
1. Enlarge and tighten every right strip, one idea per frame, starting with frame 4. Cut content before adding anything. This fixes thumbnail legibility, dead panel space, and half the AI look at once.
2. Fix the sample-state arithmetic in frames 1 and 2, 30m with 2 drops, and a lock state a stranger can reconcile. Contradictory numbers are the fastest way to look machine-assembled.
3. Label frame 1's invented video as sample, or remove its view count and channel metrics. This is the only fix with rejection risk attached.
4. Redraw the frame 1 thumbnail and the tile drop with specific, less glossy detail. These are the two images a search-list viewer actually sees, and both carry the strongest stock and AI signals.
5. De-symmetrise the copy. Cut "Water rises as your watch time runs out," finish "Choose how strict," and let "Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen" stand as the only antithetical headline in the set. One is a voice, two is a template.
