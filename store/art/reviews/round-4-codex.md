1. Home page

Design: The single video makes the product understandable quickly. The white Watch button and blue/gold tokens provide useful contrast. The illustration occupies most of the panel’s visual attention, while the reveal action is comparatively quiet.

AI look: The layered mountain illustration has a familiar stock-vector feel. The repeated dark marketing frame adds some template character. The specific controls and restrained branding keep that impression low.

What works: “One video at a time.” is the strongest headline in the set. The drawing fits the quiet tone. Keep it.

Fixes, highest impact first:

1. Explain the card more directly. Replace “A card reveals each new video.” with “Spend a card to reveal the next video.” That connects the unfamiliar token to the visible action.
2. Increase separation between the cyclist and the dark hillside in the illustration. The cyclist becomes a small black knot when reduced.
3. Align this caption block’s top with the other four. Its single-line headline currently starts noticeably lower.

2. Lock screen

Design: The modal has a clear hierarchy, and Done for now is the strongest action. The default hour makes the example easy to understand. The background is atmospheric, but the paused-video context is almost lost.

AI look: The underwater glow and bubbles could resemble a generic wellness graphic in isolation. The concrete time, token cost, and weekly allowance make it feel like a specific product.

What works: It shows an actual stopping point without scolding. The spare-drop option does not contradict the hour watched. The still image works without the moving light.

Fixes, highest impact first:

1. Replace “The lock comes up.” with “Take a break.” The current line describes a mechanism; the replacement makes the purpose clear.
2. Shorten the subline to “Limits are optional. Use a spare drop for 15 more minutes, or stop here.”
3. Adjust the sample illustration’s composition so a recognizable piece of the trail remains visible around the modal. Currently, someone seeing this alone could mistake it for a separate relaxation screen.

3. Mode choice

Design: The three stacked options communicate choice clearly. The blue selected border is easy to find. However, this is the most text-heavy frame, with a marketing headline, page heading, instructions, descriptions, badge, and link all competing.

AI look: Three rounded option cards with a recommended tier are a familiar onboarding template. The small interface previews and specific limits give it more intent than a generic pricing graphic.

What works: The screenshot proves that limits are optional. The selected Full drip mode connects well to the following Settings image.

Fixes, highest impact first:

1. Change “Three levels. Switch any time.” to “Choose how much drip does.” The interface explicitly distinguishes when changes apply during the first 24 hours. “Switch any time” invites an assumption of immediate changes afterward.
2. Shorten the subline to “Search only, one video at a time, or daily limits.”
3. Tighten the captured-page crop modestly around the options and Next button. The introductory UI text is secondary at store size. Preserve all three choices.

4. Settings

Design: The controls look credible and usable. The blue switches stand out. As a listing image, it spends substantial space showing configuration details that become hard to read when reduced.

AI look: This reads primarily as a real settings screen. The shared frame is conventional, but there is little synthetic-looking decoration.

What works: The image substantiates customization. Showing both the daily allowance and overnight schedule gives the feature practical meaning.

Fixes, highest impact first:

1. Replace “Each drop is 15 minutes of watching.” The visible Minutes per drop control makes that sound more fixed than it is. Use “Choose your daily allowance and minutes per drop.”
2. Replace “Choose the hours drops can’t be used.” with “Set overnight hours for a break.” It is shorter and avoids requiring the viewer to decode token rules.
3. Enlarge the captured settings area slightly, preserving the Mode selector through Ends at. Reduce unused space above the tabs before sacrificing any controls.

The set

The first two images tell the core story: fewer videos to choose from, then a stopping point. Keep that order. The weakness is that optional limits are explained only in the smaller subline of image 2. Someone skimming headlines could assume the lock is mandatory until image 3.

The five frames share a strong visual system: navy background, white headlines, muted sublines, consistent left margin, consistent small logo, and the same outer panel origin. The right and bottom bleed looks intentional.

The captured pages have different visual densities. Image 1 centers a relatively narrow video card; image 2 spreads atmosphere around a modal; image 3 fills the panel with choices; image 4 has a broad settings layout; image 5 nearly fills the height. Some variation helps distinguish features. The History card’s tight bottom margin and image 1’s lower caption start are the clearest alignment issues.

At thumbnail size, the headlines carry the story. Image 1’s video, image 2’s modal, and image 5’s bars remain recognizable. Image 3 becomes three gray slabs; image 4 becomes rows and switches. Their detailed text will not carry meaning there. The small drip marks function as recognition cues, not readable labels. Tile c holds up better than d3.

No caption strongly reads as AI-written. “See where your time went.” is generic analytics copy, but it accurately describes the image. “Three levels. Switch any time.” sounds like standard software marketing and is the weakest claim. “The lock comes up.” is awkward rather than AI-like. “One video at a time.” is specific enough to keep.

Rejection and misleading-content concerns

The supplied dimensions match Chrome’s screenshot and small-tile specifications. The outer canvases have square corners and backgrounds that reach the edges. Google permits explanatory visual aids and asks that screenshots show current functionality without overwhelming text. I see no clear image-format violation. [Chrome listing guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)

The visible brand is drip. There is no YouTube logo or apparent endorsement claim. Google’s trademark guidance still applies to the YouTube reference and listing description; its guidance calls for attribution. The unseen listing text cannot be assessed here. [Chrome branding guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/branding)

The main accuracy concerns are “Switch any time” and the fixed-sounding 15-minute statement beside a configurable control. The History improvement reads as sample usage, not a promised outcome. The supplied explanation of the lock capture gives no reason to flag its frozen bubbles or sample hour as misleading.
