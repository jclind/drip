1. screenshot-1.png

The strongest product introduction. The headline and single video communicate the central idea immediately. The blue and gold accents give the dark interface some character without taking over.

The illustration’s broad mountain layers and dark palette look somewhat like stock vector scenery. That slightly raises the AI look score, but the specific controls and restrained composition feel intentional.

Fixes, by impact:
- Explain the card in the frame subline. “Replace YouTube’s feed. One card reveals one video.” connects the unfamiliar token to the main action.
- Increase contrast within the illustration, especially around the cyclist and ridge. Keep the drawn thumbnail. Its subject currently disappears at small size.
- Consider a modest enlargement of the captured page. The watch-time display and action labels lose detail before the headline does.

2. screenshot-2.png

The large modal and two clear choices communicate a stopping point. The white “Done for now” button gives stopping appropriate visual weight.

The water effect is the weakness. In this still, it reads mostly as a blue glow behind a black dialog. The clipped bubbles on the right and faint horizontal edges make the capture feel less finished. These look like capture issues, not strong evidence of AI generation.

Fixes, by impact:
- Choose a sample animation state with a more visible water boundary. Keep the bubbles inside the crop. Make the rising-water idea readable without motion.
- Replace “The lock comes up” with a concrete result: “When time runs out, / the video stops.”
- Shorten the subline to: “Limits are optional. One drop adds 15 minutes, or you can stop for the day.”

The default “1h watched today” is coherent with the stated sample. Freezing animation for a still is reasonable.

This is also the most template-like frame: dark rounded cards, miniature diagrams, recommendation badge, white next button. That raises the AI look score slightly, although the content remains specific.

Fixes, by impact:

The small text inside the captured page will not survive thumbnail size. The caption must carry the distinction.

Across the set

Story and order

The first two images explain “one video” followed by “a stopping point.” That is a clear story, but it leads strongly with restriction. Search-only use appears later and never gets a substantial demonstration.

I would use 1, 3, 2, 4, 5. Show the central experience, establish the available modes, then explain the optional lock. This better supports the product’s stated flexibility.

Consistency

The navy background, left caption position, panel origin, white headings, and bottom-left mark are consistent.

The captured pages vary more:
- Settings and mode choice fill the panel width.
- Home and History sit farther inside it.
- History uses visibly smaller interface text.
- Screenshot 2 appears to end its panel above the canvas bottom, while the others continue through the bottom edge.

Use a common apparent text scale where practical. Keep the outer panel treatment consistent. Different pages do not need identical content positions, but the current differences feel partly accidental.

Thumbnail legibility

The headlines and blue accents survive. Most sublines and interface explanations do not.

Image 1 retains a recognizable video card. Image 2 retains a modal and two buttons. Image 5 retains a chart. Images 3 and 4 become stacks of controls whose meaning depends almost entirely on their captions.

Tile c is the strongest at small size. Tile d loses its differentiating line. Tile e keeps its colors but loses the card’s meaning.

Copy

Nothing strongly reads as AI-written. The weaker lines are vague or overworked:
- “The lock comes up” describes the mechanism awkwardly.
- “Choose your drops and dry hours” puts brand vocabulary ahead of comprehension.
- “See your week” is generic.
- “Daily watch time against the week before, and the drops and cards you used” reads like a compressed feature inventory.

“One video at a time” is familiar, but the adjacent image earns it.

Rejection and misleading impressions

No obvious rejection trigger is visible. The stated dimensions match Google’s screenshot and small-tile guidance. The outer images are rectangular and filled; rounded panels inside the artwork are a separate design element. Google also emphasizes accurate current functionality, readable captures, and restrained text. [Chrome Web Store image guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)

There is no visible YouTube logo treatment implying endorsement. The illustrated thumbnail avoids showing recognizable creator artwork. Textual mention of YouTube describes the extension’s context.

The History decrease is presented as an individual sample, not a promised outcome. Keep it that way. The lock demonstration is consistent with the supplied explanation; the still does not need to reproduce motion.

I assessed the attached images and checked Google’s image guidance. I did not inspect code, run the extension, or verify file encoding.
