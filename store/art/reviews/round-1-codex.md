Image 1

The single video gives the image a clear subject. The headline is immediate, and the blue and gold accents have useful jobs. The dark illustration loses contrast against the surrounding page. Its layered mountains and broad cloud shapes give it a slightly generic vector look, which raises the AI-look score.

What works: the absence of competing videos demonstrates the product’s restraint. The illustrated thumbnail still reads as a video, helped by the title and duration.

Fixes, in order:
1. Name YouTube in the frame copy. “The home feed” assumes visitors already know what drip changes. Suggested subline: “Replace YouTube’s home feed with one video, revealed when you ask.”
2. Improve separation inside the illustration. Lighten the trail and distinguish the cyclist from the hills. Keep the drawn treatment.
3. Tighten the captured-page crop slightly. The video and its actions should carry more weight than the empty area around the upper counters.

Image 2

The large modal and two clear actions make this readable. The blue background adds atmosphere, but the rising-water idea barely registers. The glowing bubble touching the white button looks like a cursor effect or capture artifact. Those details lower design quality more than they suggest AI generation.

What works: “1h watched today” gives the limit a concrete meaning. The default-day sample is coherent. The image makes the optional stopping point understandable.

Fixes, in order:
1. Choose a sample animation state and crop that expose the water boundary against recognizable video context. The current background reads mainly as blue haze.
2. Move the staged glowing bubble away from the button edge. It interferes with the action’s outline.
3. Replace “The lock comes up.” It describes mechanics awkwardly. “Out of minutes? / Time to pause.” is cleaner.
4. Replace “a drop buys” with “use a drop for” in the subline. “Buys” introduces an unnecessary suggestion of payment.

Image 3

The selected blue outline creates a strong focal point. The three choices are visibly distinct, and the caption communicates a useful progression. The image is text-heavy, though. The small preview diagrams become nearly meaningless in thumbnails. The repeated rounded cards feel conventional rather than conspicuously AI-made.

What works: it establishes that limits are optional and that search-only use is a real choice. That matters after the lock image.

Fixes, in order:
1. Shorten the frame subline: “Hide the feeds, reveal one video at a time, or add a daily limit.” The current version makes viewers learn three mode names while also reading those names inside the page.
2. Try “Search only, / or add limits.” as the headline. “Go further” is vague and suggests that more restriction is necessarily better.
3. Keep all three choices in the crop. Enlarging only the selected option would weaken the point of this image.

Image 4

The headline is direct. The repeated blue switches and aligned controls give the settings page structure. But the right panel contains many equally weighted details. At reduced size, it becomes a column of controls with little readable meaning. The plain, functional interface keeps the AI-look score low.

What works: it provides evidence of customization. The four-drop, fifteen-minute sample also supports the one-hour default shown elsewhere.

Fixes, in order:
1. Focus the crop on Mode, Watch time, and Dry hours. The lower exception controls add density without strengthening “Set your own limits.”
2. Qualify the subline. Image 3 explicitly shows a first-24-hours exception, so “Looser ones wait until tomorrow” is too absolute across this set. Suggested copy: “After your first day, stricter limits apply now. Looser ones wait until tomorrow.”
3. Give the focused capture a clear bottom margin. The current page continues beyond the frame, which makes this feel more like a viewport excerpt than a composed feature image.

The set

Order

The first two images tell “choose one video, then stop when your time runs out.” That is a coherent opening.

They do not clearly tell “this changes YouTube.” The first image’s drawn thumbnail and custom page remove most familiar platform cues. YouTube appears only in small interface text in image 3.

Keep the current order if optional limits are a major selling point. Make “Limits are optional” more prominent in image 2’s frame copy so the lock does not define the product as mandatory restriction.

Consistency

The navy background, left caption position, panel origin, typography, and bottom-left mark form a consistent system. The caption blocks appear vertically balanced despite their different line counts.

The captured content is less consistent:
- Image 1 has generous space around a relatively narrow focal area.
- Image 2 centers a large modal.
- Image 3 fills the panel broadly.
- Image 4 presents a dense, continuing settings page.
- Image 5 nearly touches the bottom edge.

Those differences partly reflect the real pages. Harmonize the visible margins and focal size without forcing every page into an identical crop.

Small-size legibility

The headlines and promo tile hold up best. The video in image 1, modal in image 2, selection outline in image 3, and blue bars in image 5 remain recognizable.

Most interface descriptions and frame sublines will disappear in small carousel thumbnails. Image 4 suffers most because its meaning depends on reading labels. Each headline therefore needs to name the feature clearly by itself.

Copy

No line strongly signals AI authorship. The weaker lines are ordinary generic marketing language:
- “or go further” is vague.
- “See where the time went” is familiar category copy.
- “The lock comes up” sounds like internal implementation language.
- “a drop buys” can imply a purchase.

“One video at a time” and “Set your own limits” are plain, specific enough, and easy to skim.

Listing risks and misleading impressions

The stated dimensions match Chrome’s screenshot and small-tile specifications. The supplied compositions have square outer corners and backgrounds that fill the canvas. Rounded panels inside the artwork are a separate design choice. Chrome also advises clear screenshots with limited text, which is most relevant to images 3 and 4. [Chrome image guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)

I see no obvious YouTube logo misuse or suggestion of official affiliation. The drawn thumbnail avoids presenting a recognizable creator’s thumbnail.

The tour-rendered lock is not inherently misleading given your explanation that it runs the real lock code. The one-hour sample is coherent. More material concerns are the unqualified settings-delay claim and “buys,” which may confuse expectations. Chrome requires listing imagery to represent the product accurately. [Listing requirements](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/listing-requirements/)

The history reduction reads as one sample week, not a promised result. Keep it that way.
