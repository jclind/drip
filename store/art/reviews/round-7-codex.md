Image 1: Home

The headline communicates the main benefit immediately. The cream and black split gives the product a distinct identity. The illustration supports the quiet tone and does not look obviously AI-generated.

The subline is awkward: “one pick from it” makes the reader resolve what “it” means. The first annotation then repeats much of that explanation. Meanwhile, the thumbnail dominates the right side more than the one-video controls do.

Fixes, in impact order, to reach 9:

- Replace the subline with “Replace YouTube’s home feed with one recommendation at a time.”
- Shorten the first note to “From your own recommendations.”
- Scope the second note to the mode shown: “In Full drip, each new pick uses a card.”

Keep the drawn thumbnail. Its simple shapes are coherent; adding detail would not solve the communication problems. AI look already meets the target.

Image 2: Lock

The large lock makes the consequence clear. “Limits are optional” is essential reassurance and appears early enough. The water gives the feature some character without competing with the lock.

The lock text appears softer than the frame text. The fully raised water and heavy tint make the paused video difficult to recognize, so the right side can initially resemble a separate meditation app.

Fixes, in impact order, to reach 9:

- Capture the lock at its final display scale so its text stays crisp. Preserve the intended blur on the underlying video.
- Shorten the annotations to “One spare drop adds 15 minutes.” and “Give a reason to unlock. Up to 3 times a week.”
- If adjusting the sample background, position a recognizable part of the ridge illustration in an exposed area. Keep the full water state.

The spare drop after an hour is consistent with the supplied carryover explanation. The restrained bubbles and matching illustration keep AI look at 2.

Image 3: Modes

This establishes that drip offers a choice rather than imposing a limit. The selected Full drip card gives the capture a clear focal point. The small mode illustrations look deliberate.

There are too many competing explanations: the frame subline, two annotations, three option descriptions, and the page’s introductory copy. The frame should make the interface easier to scan.

Fixes, in impact order, to reach 9:

- Set the token note on two clear lines: “A drop gives 15 minutes.” / “A card reveals one video.”

Keep all three options and the Next button visible. Cropping to Full drip would undermine the point of this frame. The repeated card layout belongs to the real interface and does not make the image look artificially generated.

Image 4: Settings

The first annotation points effectively to the daily quantity. The second leaves out the most consequential fact: watching stops. “Minutes left go back as whole drops” also requires the reader to understand the token system already. The visible interface phrase “Paid watching stops” can suggest money.

Fixes, in impact order, to reach 9:

- Change the headline to “Set your daily limit.” It better matches the controls and avoids making four drops sound mandatory.
- Replace the lower note with “At 11pm, watching pauses. Unused time returns as whole drops.” Use two lines and keep its leader on Dry hours.
- Qualify the subline to match image 3’s first-day exception: “After day one: stricter changes now, looser changes tomorrow.”
- Shorten the first note to “Four 15-minute drops: one hour a day.”

The straight alignment and restrained controls support AI look 2. Its main problem is what the frame chooses to explain, rather than an artificial visual style.

The set

Order: The first two images tell the central story: one recommendation, then an optional stopping point. That works. Search-only mode arrives in image 3, which is reasonable. Keep the order; improve the wording rather than rearranging the carousel.

Consistency: The shared split at x=560, cream background, headline position, blue leaders, and footer mark create a strong system. The different headline lengths do not need equalizing. Right-side density varies more: image 1 has a large illustration, image 2 a single dominant lock, image 3 three stacked choices, image 4 substantial dark space, and image 5 a nearly full-height panel. Preserve different capture scales where they help readability, but bring the outer vertical clearances closer together.

Small-size legibility: The headlines, drop mark, lock silhouette, and chart survive. Most annotation text and interface descriptions will disappear in small carousel thumbnails. At approximately 320×200, the 20-pixel frame notes become roughly 5-pixel text, and the thin leaders become fragile. The thumbnails therefore communicate “video, pause, choices, settings, history,” rather than the detailed mechanics. That is sufficient if the full-size captions stay concise. Tile p is the safer small-format choice.

Copy: Nothing reads unmistakably AI-written. “See where the time went” is familiar and slightly generic, but appropriate. “When the time runs out” is also conventional, though the lock makes it specific. The weaker lines are awkward rather than synthetic:

- “one pick from it”
- “or a daily limit on top”
- “minutes left go back as whole drops”
- “see what it used”

Replace those before rewriting the stronger headlines.

Rules and misleading impressions: The supplied dimensions match Google’s screenshot and small promo sizes, and five screenshots fit its maximum. The compositions visibly fill their canvases.
