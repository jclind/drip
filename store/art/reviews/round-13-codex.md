Image 1

The product premise lands immediately. The cream panel, large serif headline, and dark page make a clear hierarchy. The token legend connects the cartoon objects to useful functions. The drawn thumbnail works as an example video and should stay.

What holds it below 9: the thumbnail dominates the product controls, while the explanation is spread across three widely separated areas. At thumbnail size, viewers get a landscape and a headline; the mechanism disappears.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. Tighten the legend to “One drop = 15 minutes.” and “One card = one new video.” This improves scanning and removes repeated sentence structure.
2. Shorten the bottom note to “Reveal another video for one card.” It names the action directly.
3. Move that leader’s endpoint closer to the reveal icon. It currently terminates above the button, making the connection weaker than it needs to be.

AI look stays low because the annotations follow specific controls and the sample content is coherent. The smooth vector landscape is the most stock-like element, but it does not make the whole frame look generated.

The set

The order tells the core story within two images: replace endless browsing with one pick, then stop when the chosen time ends. Keep that opening.

The optional nature of time limits arrives too late. Add “Optional daily watch-time limits.” to image 2’s frame copy, perhaps as its subline’s first sentence. That prevents the opening pair from implying every mode stops playback.

Images 3, 4, and 5 then answer sensible follow-up questions: how much control, how changes take effect, and what happened over time. The search-only option is explained but never demonstrated. That is a coverage gap, though it does not justify weakening the first two images.

Consistency is strong in the fixed panel split, left margin, mark position, headline treatment, and blue annotations. Different page densities are the main inconsistency. Settings looks compact and technical; History fills nearly its entire panel; the home page has one large visual anchor. Do not force identical interface scale across those pages. Preserve the frame alignment and shorten the surrounding explanation.

The empty space mostly supports the calm tone. Image 3 has the least productive gap between its subline and notes. Do not fill it with more copy.

At small thumbnail size, headlines, the landscape, lock, selected mode outline, and chart survive. Notes, legends, and most interface text do not. At half size, a 20-pixel note becomes 10 pixels; in a smaller thumbnail it becomes closer to 5. The annotation system should reward opening the image, while the headline and main visual carry the thumbnail.

No caption clearly reads as AI-written. “Or set your own mix.” is the most generic line. “Choose how strict.” is vague alone, but the mode choices explain it. “Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.” and “Your week in minutes.” are specific and worth keeping.

Rules and misleading impressions

The stated dimensions match Chrome’s screenshot and small-tile sizes. Annotated frames are supported by its guidance on visual aids. The cream panels are designed content, so I would not treat them as outer padding. Chrome also advises against excessive screenshot text and asks that tiles work at half size. [Chrome listing guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)

I see no obvious rejection trigger in the visible content. The main accuracy risk is presenting optional limits as universal. Keep the sample label in History. Its improvement figures should remain clearly illustrative because listing images must accurately describe the product. [Listing requirements](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/listing-requirements/)

There is no visible YouTube logo or claim of official affiliation. However, “YouTube” is the largest brand name in image 1. For a more conservative treatment, use “One video at a time.” as the headline and identify compatibility in the subline, such as “A Chrome extension for YouTube™.” Chrome’s branding guidance calls for compatibility wording and trademark attribution.
