AI-look scores describe appearance, not authorship.

Image 1: screenshot-1.png

The strong headline, single video, and restrained palette make the core idea clear. The blue and gold tokens add character without taking over. The geometric landscape is the most template-like element, particularly the stacked mountains and cloud shapes. It still looks deliberate.

What works: the video dominates the captured page. “One video at a time” is the strongest headline in the set. The illustration’s subject fits the product’s quieter mood.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. “A single video you chose to see” implies the viewer selected that particular video. Replace the subline with “drip replaces YouTube’s home feed with one recommendation at a time.”
2. The card callout ends in the gap between “Stashed” and “Reveal next.” Point it directly at the Reveal next button. Change the note to “One card reveals the next video.”
3. Keep the illustration, but increase contrast around the cyclist and ridge. At small sizes, the scene becomes dark horizontal bands.

Image 2: screenshot-2.png

The large lock message and white exit button create a clear focal point. The underwater treatment gives this frame more personality than the others. Its weakness is that the water reads as a dark blue background before it reads as water covering a video. Nothing visibly suggests obvious AI generation.

What works: the lock is readable, the action hierarchy is clear, and the subdued bubbles support the tone. The spare drop after an hour is consistent with the stated carryover behavior.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. The frame copy describes motion that the still barely communicates. Replace “Water rises over the video and the lock comes up” with “Your video pauses when time is up.” Let the water provide atmosphere.
2. “Or type why you need this one. Three a week.” hides what is being counted. Use “Type a reason to unlock. Up to 3 times a week.”
3. Keep “Limits are optional” close to the headline. It is important reassurance, but currently the third line gets less attention.

The set

The first two images tell the main story: replace the feed, then stop when the optional time allowance ends. Image 1 needs the recommendation wording corrected. Image 2 gives two ways to continue substantial explanatory space, which slightly weakens the stopping message.

The current order works. Image 3 then explains that the viewer can choose a lighter mode. Keep the optional-limits reassurance prominent in image 2 so people do not leave before reaching that choice.

Consistency is strong in the cream panel, left alignment, serif headlines, blue leaders, and small brand mark. Caption heights are mostly stable. The main inconsistency is captured-page density: image 2 presents one large decision, while image 4 presents a settings inventory. The empty cream space generally feels intentional. Enlarging image 4’s relevant controls would improve balance more than filling that space with copy.

At thumbnail size, the headlines, single video, lock, selected mode outline, and blue chart bars survive. The notes, leader endpoints, settings labels, and token counts largely disappear. The main message must therefore stand without the annotations. Tile c communicates more; tile p retains larger lettering.

No frame copy sounds conspicuously AI-written. “Choose how much drip does” is vague. “See where the time went” is familiar marketing language, though the chart substantiates it. “No watching on drops overnight” is awkward jargon. “One video at a time” is concise and specific enough to keep.
