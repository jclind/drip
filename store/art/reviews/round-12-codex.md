Image 1

The strongest screenshot for explaining the product. The large video, restrained palette, and token legend establish the idea quickly. The illustration fits the product’s character. Keeping it is reasonable.

The score drops because the thumbnail occupies much more attention than the mechanism that makes drip different. At small size, viewers see a handsome video player; “Reveal next” and its card cost become secondary.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. Identify the product category in the frame subline: “A Chrome extension that replaces / YouTube’s home feed with one pick.” The current image assumes viewers already understand where drip lives.
2. Shorten the bottom annotation to “Another pick costs one card.” Start the leader immediately after it and retain the endpoint above “Reveal next.”
3. Give the token legend slightly more separation from the subline. Those three closely stacked explanations compete with the otherwise spacious composition.

Those changes would get it toward 9. AI look is already at 2. The smooth landscape illustration has a familiar vector-stock appearance, but its specific setting and controlled composition keep it from looking generated.

The set

The first two images tell the main story: one recommendation replaces the feed, then a daily limit pauses watching. That order works. The optional nature of limits becomes clear only in image 3, so naming the Chrome extension in image 1 and the configurable daily limit in image 2 would improve the opening.

The five frames are consistent in their split, cream background, serif headlines, brand position, and blue annotations. Keep those anchors. Do not force identical page scaling: the settings need detail, while the home and lock need context.

The biggest inconsistency is visual weight. Image 1 is dominated by an illustration, image 2 by a modal, and images 3–4 by much smaller interface text. Image 5 fills almost the entire right panel, while Settings has a substantial dark area above its content. A tighter Settings crop would help.

The empty space in the caption panels is mostly useful. The problem is the long leaders crossing it. With repeated use, they make the set resemble a product manual. One strong annotation per frame would feel more restrained.

At thumbnail size, the headlines, blue drop, video rectangle, modal, and chart survive. Most sublines and annotations become texture. The pending-change pill in image 4 and counter labels in image 5 are especially vulnerable. The captions must communicate the benefit without requiring those details.

No frame copy strongly reads as AI-written. “Choose how strict” is vague. “Or set your own mix” is generic. “Time for a breather” is familiar wellness language, but it belongs to the interface and fits this context. “Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.” is the strongest headline because the screenshot demonstrates a specific rule.

Store rules and misleading impressions

The stated dimensions match Chrome’s screenshot and small-tile sizes. The images have square outer corners and fill their canvases. Chrome explicitly allows explanatory visual aids, while advising against overwhelming text. These frames’ main exposure is readability, especially image 4. [Chrome listing guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)

I see no YouTube logo treatment or endorsement claim in the attachments. The drawn thumbnail avoids displaying a real creator’s thumbnail. These observations do not establish rights clearance, but there is no obvious visual impersonation issue. Chrome prohibits false affiliation and intellectual-property infringement. [Chrome Web Store policies](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/policies/)

The History result is the clearest possible misleading impression: viewers may read the decline as an advertised outcome. Moving “Sample week” beside the numbers would address that. Chrome requires screenshots and promotional material to describe the product accurately. [Listing requirements](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/listing-requirements/)

The staged lock is reasonable given the supplied explanation. Showing a real lock on sample content, freezing the animation, and hiding a motion-dependent light do not visibly promise a different feature.
