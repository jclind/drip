Across the set, the first two images tell the story: one recommendation, followed by an optional stopping point. Keep that order. Image 3 then answers how much control the user has. Settings and History are sensible supporting frames.

The shared divider at roughly x=560, left text edge, headline style, blue leaders, and bottom brand mark make the five frames consistent. The crop and scale are less consistent. Image 1’s recommendation is relatively inset; image 2’s lock nearly fills its panel; image 4 carries substantial empty space above its controls; image 5 fills almost the full height. Different subjects justify different crops, but image 4 needs tightening most. Do not force identical zoom levels at the expense of readable content.

The captions and sublines are consistently brief. The left-side empty space generally supports the restrained tone. It becomes less useful where tiny annotations sit far apart, especially in images 1 and 4.

At small thumbnail size, the headlines, blue accents, lock, and chart survive. Most annotations and interface detail do not. At one-quarter scale, 16px notes become roughly 4px text. The images therefore need to work through headline and silhouette first. Larger annotations will help the carousel view, but will not make every detail readable in a thumbnail.

No caption reads conspicuously as AI-written. “See where the time went” is familiar marketing copy, though the chart gives it substance. The weaker phrases are vague or awkward rather than AI-like:

- “Revealed from your own home feed”: use “From your own YouTube recommendations.”
- “One card reveals the next video”: use “One card reveals another video.” “The next” can suggest a fixed queue.
- “No clock, and no cost per video”: use explicit limit language.
- “Stricter settings” and “Looser ones”: use lower and higher limits.
- “Its drops and cards here”: remove “here” and name watch time too.
