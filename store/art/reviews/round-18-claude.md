2. Overall pick: V

Ship V as a whole set. Every claim matches the product, every image shows enough of the page to understand it, and the leader lines tie the copy to specific controls. That's what a store visitor scanning five thumbnails needs. A is the more distinctive set and wins two images on its own merits. Dropping A3 or A5 into V would still break the system, though: two layouts and two type scales in one carousel looks like indecision. I wouldn't mix. V's weakness is that a split caption panel next to a screenshot is the most common store layout there is. Section 4 is mostly about breaking that template feel.

4. What would get each V image to design 9 and AI look 2, highest impact first

Across the set:
- The template tell is that all five use the same 560/720 split with the headline, subline, and notes in the same place. Image 2 shows the fix: one drawn element crosses the seam. Give every image one. In 1, a drop falls from the logo toward the counter. In 4, the leader becomes a short hand-drawn arrow. In 5, the chart's dashed 48m line extends into the cream panel and ends at the note. Keep it to one element per image, drawn in the drop's blue.
- The vertical rhythm leaves empty cream between the subline (about y300) and the notes (y440 to y700) in 1, 3, and 5. Either move each note to the height of its target and let the empty space be deliberate, or increase the headline size about 15% so the panel has weight.

Image 1:
- The token legend ("A drop is 15 minutes...", "A card reveals...") floats at mid-height and points at nothing. Move it to the top, level with the counter, and run two short leaders to the "2" and the "7" next to the drop and card icons. Then the image explains the tokens on screen instead of beside it.
- The elbowed leader to Reveal next runs under the Watch button area. Route it straight across at y736 to the left edge of the Reveal next button.

Image 2:
- The brief says blue water rises. In the panel it's dark teal, and on the page side it's only a blurred gradient with bubbles and no surface line. Run one continuous water surface across both halves at the same height (around y630), in a blue close to the drop's.
- "Set a daily limit" reads as mandatory. "Set a daily limit if you want one" or "With a daily limit on, ..." keeps it honest at no cost.

Image 3:
- The headline duplicates the page's own question. Give the subline the selling point instead, for example "Start strict, or just hide the feed." Add a third note at the Quiet card, "Search only, nothing else," so the notes cover the choice and not only the default.

Image 4:

Image 5:

Tile:

That makes the tile about the product's single idea. Or set the gold card beside the drop so both tokens appear. Keep "One video at a time." and set "drip" slightly smaller so the drop leads.

5. What cost the other two sets the most

A: the slab crop hides the product. The page enters from the top right and loses its top and right edges. A1 loses the 34m counter and the top of the thumbnail, and A3 loses the Next button. Meanwhile the numeral takes about 40% of the canvas. The copy is also forced to read as "numeral + period + phrase," which produces "1h. then the water comes up." The ring isn't consistent either: it's on 1, 2, and 5 but missing on 3 and 4.

B: the shapes look arbitrary and the type looks default. The flag-and-bar "1" in B1, the stretched oval in B2, and the slab "7" in B5 read as random black and blue blocks rather than symbols. The bold grotesk looks like system Arial. All of it fights the "restraint should feel good" tone. B5 also shows an empty dark strip on the left of the page panel, which looks like a rendering mistake.

6. Rejection or misleading risks

- None of the sets shows a YouTube logo, and the drawn thumbnail avoids third-party content. V1's "A Chrome extension for YouTube" is nominative use, which is fine.
- V1, A1, and B1 say the next pick costs a card. In Curated mode, new videos come "as many as you want," so the card cost only applies in Full drip. That's a minor overclaim on the first image a visitor sees. Consider "With Full drip, the next pick costs a card," or rely on the mode screen.
- A2's "1h" headline could read as drip imposing a one-hour cap. The subline "Limits are optional" saves it, but it's small and gray. V2's "Set a daily limit" has the same softer issue.
- The History sample data is labeled in all three: V5 in the subline, A5 and B5 explicitly. The numbers check out. 41 against 48 is a 14.6% drop, which rounds to 15%, and Saturday, Nov 7 2026 really is a Saturday. 30 minutes equals 2 drops of 15.
- The B tile uses a white drop, while every screenshot uses blue. If the extension's icon is the blue drop, the tile won't match the icon next to it in the store.
- Nothing else looks like grounds for rejection. The claims about tightening and loosening are correct in V4, A4, and B4.
