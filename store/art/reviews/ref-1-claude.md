The median is 7 for design and 4 for AI look.

The Adobe frames (08-10) and the NordPass frames (11-13) are the strongest. Each makes one claim and shows one piece of UI, big enough to read at carousel size. Each set sticks to one color world, and there are no floating stickers. NordPass repeats a strict layout of tag pill, big headline, and short subline next to a device. Adobe uses only a headline and lets real photos carry the frame. The Monica frames (01-07) lose points for 3D sparkles, a lightning bolt, a lightbulb, AI-generated imagery, pills scattered on the screen, and a cursor that covers "Gemini 3 Pro". Ref 03's "Unleash... stunning" copy reads as templated. One small slip in ref 10: "FIle Type" has a typo, and it still looks like a designer made it.

Text load: Adobe uses about 3 to 5 words per frame, a headline and nothing else. NordPass uses about 15 to 30 words: a one-word pill, a 4 to 7 word headline, and a 10 to 20 word subline. Monica uses about 20 to 40 words of marketing copy plus labels. None of the 13 uses leader-line callouts. The typical reference carries one headline, at most one subline, and zero to three short labels.

Overall, the set's AI look beats most of the Monica frames. The cream panel, serif headline, and blue leader lines hold together as one system across all five frames. The cream-and-serif editorial style is a common look right now, which is the only reason AI look isn't a 2. The real weakness is word count. Every frame carries 25 to 45 words of caption on top of the UI's own text, which puts it at Monica's level, and the clean references use half that or less.

1. Screenshot 1, above the middle. The headline is short and true, the illustration is clearly drawn by a person, and the counter at the top of the strip ties straight to the drop and card legend. The left panel has a dead band between the subline and the legend, which is minor.

2. Screenshot 2, below the middle. Three things pull it down. First, the right half is mostly soft color blobs with a dark navy smudge at the top left, which can read as a rendering glitch rather than "a paused video under water". Second, the drawn wave band stops dead at the seam (see Q2). Third, the frame carries two notes that repeat the headline and the button. To bring it up: remove the cream panel's water band and its note "At 0 minutes left, the video pauses under the water", and put the pause fact in the subline, for example "An optional daily limit. When it's used up, the video pauses." The blur is fixed, so the strip itself can't change much. A slightly smaller strip scale, so the lock panel sits centered with more bubble-dotted water around it, would make the water read better than the gray top does now.

3. Screenshot 3, at the middle. The real UI does the work. The card descriptions are about 15px gray on dark, so they will be hard to read when the store shrinks the carousel. The left panel has too much copy for what it adds (see Step 3).

4. Screenshot 4, at the middle. "Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen." is the best headline in the set because it names a real, unusual product rule. The subline relies on "reset", which no frame defines, and the bottom half of the cream panel is empty, so the frame looks top-heavy.

5. Screenshot 5, at the middle. The chart is clean and the sample data is consistent: the bars average about 40 to 41m, the dashed line sits at 48m, and 48 to 41 is 15%. The note is the weakest in the set (see Step 3).

6. Tile, at the middle for what a tile has to do. The drop and wordmark are recognizable at small size. The tagline's regular weight and the thin wave stripe are the parts that degrade first (see Q3).

Verdict

Ship: yes, once the item below is checked.

Blocking fixes:
- Screenshot 2 may contradict itself. In screenshot 1, "34m left today" sits next to 2 drops, which reads as minutes left including unspent drops (4m + 2 x 15m). If that reading is right, then "0 minutes left" means 0 drops left, yet the lock in screenshot 2 offers "+15m · -1 drop" and the note says "spend a drop for 15 more minutes". "1h watched today" equals the default 4 drops, which deepens the question. If the product really offers a drop at that point, for example because the lock comes up at the end of each drop and not at the end of the day, change the caption to say when the lock actually appears ("When a drop runs out..." or similar) and drop "At 0 minutes left". If my reading of the counter is wrong, nothing needs fixing. Either way, a buyer who does the math will stall here, so it's worth five minutes.

I found nothing else that looks broken or makes a wrong claim.

STEP 3: OWNER'S QUESTIONS

Q1. Text, frame by frame

Screenshot 1.
- Keep the headline "One video at a time."
- Shorten the subline to "Replaces YouTube's home feed with one pick." The listing already says it's a Chrome extension.
- Keep both legend lines. They define the drop and the card, and the whole set depends on those two words.
- Cut the note "Not this one? The next pick costs a card." The card legend line plus the button's own "Reveal next · -1 [card]" already say it. A buyer loses nothing.

Screenshot 2.
- Keep the headline.
- Keep the subline but extend it to carry the pause: "An optional daily limit. When it's used up, the video pauses." The word "optional" matters to buyers, so keep it.
- Shorten "Stop here, or spend a drop for 15 more minutes." to "Or spend a drop for 15 more minutes." Or cut it, since the button reads "+15m · -1 [drop]". I lean toward keeping the short version, because the leader line is what tells a buyer the lock isn't a hard wall.
- Cut "At 0 minutes left, the video pauses under the water." along with the band. It restates the headline, and "0 minutes left" is the phrase behind the blocking question above.

Screenshot 3.
- Keep the headline.
- Cut the subline "Start with Full drip, or choose a lighter mode." The strip's own "How much should drip do?", the three cards, and the "Recommended" badge already say it.
- Cut "The default: 4 drops and 10 cards a day." The UI says "1h of watching and 10 new videos a day", which is plainer than the token version. The note only translates plain words into jargon.
- Keep "Or set your own mix." The Customize link is small and easy to miss, and this is the one fact the UI doesn't make obvious.
- That leaves one headline and one note, which is NordPass-level text.

Screenshot 4.
- Keep the headline.
- Cut the subline "After your first 24 hours: tighten now, loosen at reset." "Reset" is undefined, and the 24-hour grace period is already in screenshot 3's UI ("For your first 24 hours, changes apply right away."). If you want a subline, use plain words: "Lower limits apply now. Higher ones wait until tomorrow."
- Keep the note "Raised to 5 drops. Today stays at 4 until 4:00am." It explains a chip that would otherwise be cryptic, and it's the concrete proof of the headline.

Screenshot 5.
- Keep the headline.
- Shorten the subline to "A sample week." The label has to stay. The numbers repeat the chart's "41m", "15% from the week before", and "48m" word for word.
- Cut the note "Saturday: 30 minutes, exactly 2 drops." It tells a buyer nothing they need, and "exactly" sounds odd. Without it, the chart stands on its own the way Adobe's frames do.
- Minor, not blocking: Friday's bar is about 76m, over both the default 60m and the raised 75m. That's plausible with "Finish video when time runs out" or an unlock. The Saturday tiles show "Unlocks used 0", so nobody will check Friday, but you could trim Friday to about 60m to be safe.

Q2. Screenshot 2's water band

No, it doesn't read as deliberate. The crest stops exactly at the seam, so it looks like a layer that got clipped, and the strip beside it has no surface at that height, only a gradual blur. It reads as decoration that belongs to the caption panel. Next to a frame about real UI, it suggests something failed to line up.

Remove it. The alternatives are worse:
- Ending it flat turns it into a teal footer bar that means nothing.
- Continuing a drawn surface over the strip would put a fake element on real UI, which breaks your own rule. It would also contradict the real lock, whose water is all the way up.
- Removing it makes the frame match the other four. The bubbles in the strip already carry the water idea, and the bottom note goes with the band.

Q3. Tile tagline

It's readable at 440x280. The problem is the store's small placements, where the tile renders at roughly half that size. The regular-weight serif has thin strokes, and "at a time." will turn gray and soft before the wordmark does.

My change: keep the two lines and the copy, and set them in the serif's semibold, about 10 to 15% larger. Two lines are fine because they make a block as wide as "drip". On one line the text would shrink further. Also, either thicken the bottom wave to about 2 to 3 times its current height so it reads as water at half size, or remove it. Right now it reads as a stray stripe.

STEP 4: TOP 5 FIXES, MOST IMPACT FIRST

1. Screenshot 2, the arithmetic in the captions. Confirm whether the lock can offer a drop when the day's minutes reach 0. If it can't, rewrite the caption to say when the lock actually appears, and remove "At 0 minutes left". This is the only possible wrong claim in the set.
2. Screenshot 2, the cream panel's bottom. Remove the water band and the note "At 0 minutes left, the video pauses under the water". Move the pause fact into the subline: "An optional daily limit. When it's used up, the video pauses."
3. Screenshot 5, the caption. Cut the note "Saturday: 30 minutes, exactly 2 drops." and its leader line, and shorten the subline to "A sample week."
4. Screenshot 4, the subline. Cut "After your first 24 hours: tighten now, loosen at reset." or replace it with "Lower limits apply now. Higher ones wait until tomorrow." Then move the headline block and note down so the cream panel isn't empty below y=400.
5. Tile c2, the tagline and wave. Set "One video / at a time." in the serif's semibold about 10 to 15% larger, and either thicken the bottom wave to 2 to 3 times its height or remove it.
