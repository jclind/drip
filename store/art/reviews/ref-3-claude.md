The median design score is 7.

The Adobe (08 to 10) and NordPass (11 to 13) frames are the best. Each makes one claim and shows one real piece of UI large enough to read at carousel size, often bleeding off an edge so it looks big. The background is one flat color, with no sparkles, 3D emoji or floating mascots. The weaker Monica frames (01, 03, 07) split into two or three panels, so every piece of UI shrinks below legibility. They pile on decorative sparkles, lightning and 3D icons, and their headlines are generic hype ("Unleash AI Creativity"). 05 and 06 do better because each has one dominant element.

Text load: Adobe carries a 3 to 4 word headline and nothing else. NordPass has a one-word pill, a 4 to 6 word headline and a 1 to 2 line subline, about 15 to 25 words. Monica has a headline and subline plus 3 to 9 chips or labels, 20 to 40 words outside the UI. A typical reference carries roughly 10 to 25 words of marketing text. Almost none use leader-line callouts. Only 07 has small arrow labels ("Input image").

I'd show the same video twice, before and after, as two stacked player frames.

Keep the cream panel, the logo, the headline "When time runs out." and the subline. On the right, replace the full-height strip with two 16:9 frames, each about 640x360, stacked with a 24px gap and centered vertically, on drip's dark page color.

- Top frame: the ridge-trail illustration from screenshot 1, sharp, as the playing video. Render it on drip's real watch page with the illustration standing in for the video, so drip's real time pill sits where it really goes, captured at "1m left". Add a small "Sample video." line under this frame, or in the cream panel.
- Bottom frame: the real lock, captured in a viewport of about 760x430 CSS px and scaled to fit 640x360, so the dark panel fills about 80% of the frame width. The blur is over that same illustration. Its colors (storm-blue top, green bottom) match the frame above, so the eye links them.
- A small down-arrow or a short gap between them is enough. No label is needed.
- The note "Or spend a drop for 15 more minutes." keeps its leader line to the "+15m · -1" pill in the bottom frame.

What it fixes: the blur gets a referent. The current frame asks the viewer to read a blur with no idea what's under it. With the sharp frame right above, the bottom one reads as "this video, paused and covered", and the 20px blur starts to explain itself instead of looking like a gradient. The frame edges make both read as video players rather than a page-wide wash. The pill at 1m tells the sequence without copy.

What it risks:
- The lock panel shrinks to about 60% of its current scale, so "Time for a breather." stays readable, but "I need this one · 3 left this week" gets small.
- Two stacked frames look more like a storyboard than the one-big-element frames that score best in the refs.
- It still doesn't show water rising. That ambition doesn't work in a still with a full-height fill, and I'd drop it. The bubbles plus the green bottom are as much water as this frame will get.
- The sharp illustration on a watch page could look like a real YouTube capture unless "Sample video." stays.
