Common to all five frames: a cream panel on the left with a serif headline and the app on the right is the most common store-screenshot template there is. That keeps every frame's AI-look score at 2 or higher. The things that pull it back toward human are the leader lines that end in a dot on a real control, the drawn thumbnail, the token legend and the water band. More of those specific touches, and fewer unpointed notes, is what moves a frame from 3 to 2.

IMAGE 1, home page with one revealed video

To get to 9 and 2: close the empty band in the caption panel, make the subline say something the caption doesn't, and balance the right panel vertically.

Pushes down:
- The caption panel is empty from the legend (about y 390) to the note (about y 750). That's about 360px of cream with nothing in it, and the note sits 36px off the bottom edge while the logo sits 58px off the top. It looks like the note was pushed down to clear the Stash button rather than placed.
- The subline repeats the headline. "One video at a time" is followed by "shows one pick at a time."
- On the right, the counter pill starts 14px from the top edge while the action row ends 72px above the bottom. The capture is top-heavy.
- The leader makes an elbow and its dot lands under the Reveal button, not on it. Every other frame uses a straight line that ends at the element.

Pushes up:
- The illustrated thumbnail is good. The storm, the ridge and the cyclist read at thumbnail size and don't look like stock or AI art.
- The two-token legend is the clearest explanation of drops and cards anywhere in the set.
- "Not this one? The next pick costs a card." is the best note copy in the set.

What works: the headline, the legend, the illustration, and the Reveal next control with its -1 card cost visible.

Fixes, by impact:
1. Subline. Rewrite so it adds information, e.g. "drip hides YouTube's home feed and shows one of your recommendations instead."
2. Note position. Raise the note so its bottom margin matches the logo's top margin (about 58px), and shorten the vertical leg so the dot touches the bottom edge of Reveal next.
3. Right-panel crop. Shift the capture down about 25px so the counter pill has about 40px of top margin and the action row has about 45px below it.
4. Empty band. Optionally move the legend down to sit between the subline and the note, so the caption panel reads as one column, not two clusters.

IMAGE 2, lock screen

Pushes down:

Pushes up:

- Carrying the water into the caption panel is a real design decision. It just isn't executed well enough yet.

What works: the lock card composition, the drop-for-minutes note, and the idea of the water band.

Fixes, by impact:

4. Subline. "Set a daily limit. When it's used up, the video pauses."

THE SET

Story in the first two: yes. 1 shows the feed replaced by one pick, plus the two tokens. 2 shows the limit. A viewer who stops after two images understands the product. Neither shows the "before" (the endless feed), so the hook depends on the headline and subline of 1 doing that work. That's another reason to fix the subline in 1.

Consistency:
- Logo, headline position, headline length (always two lines) and subline length (always two lines) are consistent. Good.
- Leaders are consistent in color and dot style. Frame 1's elbow is the odd one out.
- Unpointed notes: one in 2 (justified, it's in the water) and one in 3 (not justified).
- Panel fill varies. 1 and 5 run nearly edge to edge with tight top or bottom margins, 3 and 4 have 140 to 160px of empty top, and 2 is centered. Pick a rule, e.g. at least 40px of margin on all sides of the capture, and apply it to all five.
- Right background: 5 is visibly lighter grey than 1, 3 and 4.
- Caption panel balance: 1 and 5 leave the lower half mostly empty, 4 and 3 less so.

Legibility at thumbnail size: the headlines read in every frame. The right panels hold up in 1 (thumbnail, buttons), 2 (lock card) and 5 (bars). 3 and 4 become grey rows. The pill in 4 that proves the headline is lost. The notes, at about 19px, are borderline at thumbnail size, which is acceptable since notes are carousel-size content.

Copy that reads as written by a model or as filler:
- "Undo any time before then." (4) is vague.
- "Switch freely for the first 24 hours." (3) duplicates the UI.
- "The dashed line is the week before." (5) duplicates the UI.
- 1's subline restates its headline.
- "Choose... / Choose..." in 3.
- The rest is plain and specific. "Not this one? The next pick costs a card." and "Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen." read as human.

Rejection or misleading risk:
- No YouTube logo, red play button or YouTube UI chrome appears. "YouTube's" in sublines is descriptive use and should be fine. Keep "YouTube" off the promo tiles.
- The fake channel metadata in 1 ("Wet Gravel Club · 84K views · 1w ago") is plainly sample content beside a drawn thumbnail. Low risk.
- The numbers agree across frames: 4 drops of 15 minutes = "1h watched today," and the history averages match the chart.
- One mild claim gap: 2 says "the video pauses" and shows no recognizable video. It isn't misleading, but it isn't shown either.
- The hand-placed bubbles and the drawn water band are decoration. They don't misrepresent the UI.

Top 5 fixes across the set:
1. Screenshot 2. Match the water band's color to the right-side water and flatten its kink at the seam. Move the illustration so the ridge and cyclist show above the lock card, and replace the grey bubbles with 3 or 4 varied, bluer ones inside the frame.
2. Screenshot 1. Rewrite the subline so it doesn't repeat "one at a time." Raise the bottom note to a 58px margin with the dot touching Reveal next, and re-center the capture vertically.
3. Screenshot 5. Cut "The dashed line is the week before." and point that note at the Unlocks tile. Move "A sample week" out of the subline into a small footnote, and give the tiles about 40px of bottom margin.
4. Screenshot 3. Replace the unpointed "Switch freely for the first 24 hours." with a pointed note on the Full drip description, and change "Choose which limits apply." to "Or set your own mix."
5. Tile c3. Move the card away from the tagline (or push the text about 20px right), make the card read as a card, and enlarge the tagline about 15 percent. Then use c3.
