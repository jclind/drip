THE SET

Story: yes. Image 1 says what drip does and shows it, and image 2 shows the consequence. Someone who sees only the first two gets the product.

Consistency: headline, subline, and mark positions are identical across all five, which is good. Sublines are all two lines; image 5 adds a third small line, which is justified. Note placement follows the targets, so vertical positions vary, which is fine. Two things are inconsistent. Leaders are straight in images 2, 3, and 4 but have tiny elbows in 1 and 5. And two two-line notes (4 and 5) have leaders threading between their lines. The right strip's content edge varies a bit (right edge ≈1220 in image 1 against ≈1247 in image 4). Image 2 is the only full-bleed right panel, which works as a deliberate change of pace.

Thumbnail legibility: headlines survive at carousel-thumbnail scale, and notes don't, which is expected. Image 1 (big illustration) and image 5 (blue bars) read best small. Image 2 small is a dark card on a gray-teal smear and reads as moody but vague until the waterline is fixed. Image 4 small is a dense list.

Copy flags:
- "A Chrome extension that replaces..." (image 1): generic opener. Cut "A Chrome extension that".
- "Set to 5 drops. Stays at 4 until 4am." (image 4): redundant with the subline and the UI.
- "Add dry hours and they start tonight." (image 4): uses jargon without defining it.
- "Water rises as your watch time runs out." (image 2): fine as copy, but untrue for the still as drawn.
- "Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.": the parallel slogan is the one AI-leaning line, but it's accurate. Keep it, once.
Everything else reads as written by a person.

Rejection and misleading risk: low. Image 1's headline opens with "YouTube,". Descriptive use is generally allowed, but leading with the mark is the one place a reviewer could read implied affiliation. "Your YouTube home, one video at a time." or "One YouTube video at a time." keeps the name as a descriptor. There are no YouTube logos, and the dark player UI is generic enough. The tiles don't name YouTube, which is good. Claims match the product ("the video stops", the 4am reset, sample data disclosed). Nothing looks misleading.

Why AI look sits at 3 across the board: every frame is the same beige-left, dark-right split with a serif headline, gray subline, and thin blue leader lines ending in dots, a common Figma launch-template idiom. The human touches are the drawn thumbnail, the water band, and the token icons. To get to 2, carry one hand-made motif through all five, such as the left panel's water band at a level that tracks the story (low in image 1, risen in image 2, none in Settings), rather than adding more decoration.

Top 5 fixes across the set:
1. Image 2: freeze the water mid-rise with a visible surface at y≈630 on the right that continues the left band. This removes the gray haze and the green fragment and makes the note true.
2. Image 4: delete or rewrite note 1, which repeats the subline and the UI chip and crowds the subline, and rewrite note 2 to define dry hours.
3. Leaders, images 1, 4, and 5: start every leader past the longest line of its note, centered on the block. Make the image 1 and image 5 leaders straight, or use a clear elbow, and keep the image 5 unlocks leader off the stat card tops.
4. Image 1: cut "A Chrome extension that" from the subline, and group the legend with the subline or tie it to the counter so the column stops floating.
5. Tile: choose c2. If c3, redraw the card so it reads as a card (more of it showing, tilted) and rebalance the cluster.
