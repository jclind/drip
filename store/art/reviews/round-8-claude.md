To get to 9 and 2: explain the token counter, cut the dead band in the left panel, and give the thumbnail one hand-made detail so it stops reading as stock vector art.

Score drivers. Up: the headline is the product in five words, and the subline names YouTube's home feed, so the pitch lands on the first image. The drawn thumbnail is calm and on-tone, and the title and channel ("Riding the ridge trail before the storm", "Wet Gravel Club") sound like real YouTube. The strip crop is good. Counter, video, and action row all fit, and the -1 card on "Reveal next" matches the second note. Down: the counter at the top (2 drops, 7 cards) is the first place a viewer meets the tokens, and nothing explains it. The left panel has about 340px of empty beige between the two notes. The thumbnail is competent flat vector work, but the evenly spaced straight rain lines and the generic hill gradient read as stock illustration. The first leader ends in the black gap left of the thumbnail instead of on it.

What works: the headline, the action row with its leader, and the clean right edge.

Fixes, by impact:
1. Add a note on the counter, e.g. "Drops are minutes. Cards are new videos.", pointing at the drop and card icons. That fills the empty middle and explains tokens before screenshot 3 has to.
2. Subline "shows you one video from it": "from it" is vague. Try "drip hides YouTube's home feed and shows one video from it at a time" or "...and picks one video from it for you."
3. Thumbnail: break up the rain (vary angle and length, fewer strokes) and give the rider a little more presence, e.g. a jacket in a warm accent color, so the image has one intentional focal detail.
4. Move the first leader's dot onto the thumbnail edge or inside it.

THE SET

Story in the first two: yes. Image 1 shows the core idea and image 2 shows the lock, which is the most distinctive thing drip does. The gap is that tokens appear in image 1 unexplained and are only defined in image 3.

Consistency: the frame is consistent (paper panel, serif headline, same mark position, same leader style). The strip's vertical inset isn't. Content starts at about y=42 (1), 203 (2, card), 110 (3), 120 (4), and 30 (5). Image 3 leaves 110px empty at the bottom, while 4 and 5 run to the edge. Sublines are two lines except image 5's one line. Every frame has a large empty band in the left panel, because each note sits at its target's height. The left panel takes 44 percent of the width, which is a lot of beige for two short notes.

Legibility at thumbnail size: headlines are readable. Notes are not. Images 1, 2, and 5 are recognizable from the strip alone (thumbnail, blue-green lock, blue bars). Images 3 and 4 become indistinguishable dark lists.

Copy flags: "See where the time went." and "Pick your mode." are generic screen-time and onboarding phrases. "Your week, one bar per day." is filler that describes the chart. "Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen." uses a stock two-beat antithesis but earns it with specificity. "One spare drop" uses "spare" as jargon. "Curated has no daily limits." and the Dry hours note repeat the UI. The rest reads like a person wrote it.

AI look across the set: the cream paper background, serif display headline, and thin blue leader lines ending in dots are now a very common "tasteful default", strongly associated with AI-built landing pages. It's executed with care, which keeps the score at 3, but nothing in the frame is specific to drip except the strip. The cartoon drop and card characters, the one thing the brief says gives drip character, never appear in the frame.

Rejection or misleading risk: no YouTube logo or red play-button styling appears, and the drawn thumbnail avoids third-party content. Text mentions of YouTube are descriptive and should pass. Two claims aren't shown in the image that makes them: "The water rises" (image 2, no visible waterline) and "Slow to loosen" (image 4). Neither should cause a rejection, but both are claims without visual support. The drawn thumbnail means image 1 doesn't show what a real reveal looks like (real thumbnails). The owner accepted that, and it isn't a policy issue.

Top 5 fixes across the set:
1. Explain the tokens in image 1: add a note on the counter ("Drops are minutes. Cards are new videos.") and drop the duplicate definition from image 3.
2. Image 2: stop the water below full so a surface line shows, aim the "water rises" leader at that line, and lighten the blur enough that screenshot 1's ridge and rider are recognizable under the lock.
3. Even out the strip crops. Use a shared top inset of about 100px, scale image 5 down so it stops touching the top and bottom, and scale image 3 up to fill its empty bottom. Consider narrowing the left panel to about 480px and enlarging the strip, for thumbnail legibility.
4. Make every note add information. Replace "Curated has no daily limits." and the Dry hours note, and either show a pending looser setting in image 4 or move the "waits until tomorrow" claim into its subline.
5. Use tile-c2, swap the flat drop glyph for the cartoon drop character, align it with the text block, and bring that character into the screenshot frames once or twice (e.g. beside a headline) to break the template look.
