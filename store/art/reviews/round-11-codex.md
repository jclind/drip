1. Home page

The product is understandable at full size. The single large video proves the premise, and the token legend explains the blue and yellow without adding decorative clutter. The cream panel and dark page work well together.

What holds it below 9 is the number of separate messages: the headline, feed replacement, two token definitions, and the cost of another pick. The most important context, YouTube, appears only in the smaller subline. The bottom annotation feels detached from the explanation above it.

The drawn thumbnail has a generic vector landscape quality, which accounts for the AI-look score of 2. Its consistent shapes and restrained palette still look intentional. Keep the illustration.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. Put the platform in the headline: “YouTube, one / video at a time.” This makes the product category survive thumbnail viewing.
2. Shorten the legend to “A drop = 15 minutes.” and “A card = one new video.” The icons already supply character.
3. Shorten the bottom note to “The next pick costs a card.” Move it slightly higher and retain a leader routed below the controls. Its current position is too close to the bottom edge.

Those changes would bring this to 9 without changing the captured interface.

2. Lock screen

This is the strongest demonstration of drip’s character. The large lock, clear stopping point, and water treatment make the feature memorable. “Done for now” remains the strongest action. The saved-drop note explains the alternative without making the frame feel like an advertisement for bypassing the limit.

The blurred landscape and shaded bubbles have a more generic digital-illustration look than the other frames, hence AI look 2. They do not look obviously generated.

The main weakness is that the fully submerged background makes the paused video difficult to recognize. The frame communicates the lock more clearly than it communicates water rising over a video. The caption supplies that missing explanation.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. Optional: use a closer crop of the same paused illustration beneath the lock, with a larger rider and ridge. Preserve the real blur and tint. This could make the connection to image 1 easier to recognize.
2. Otherwise, leave it. The caption-panel water band provides enough visual explanation for a still image.

No essential change is needed to reach 9 or AI look 2.

3. Mode choice

The three choices form an immediate visual hierarchy. The selected blue outline links the interface to the brand color. The customization leader lands precisely, and the caption explains the first-day flexibility without forcing the viewer to read the interface’s smaller text.

This feels designed by a person. The repetition comes from a coherent frame system, and the annotations respond to the actual content.

The limitation is scale: the option descriptions and miniature previews will disappear in thumbnails. The large caption still carries the message, so that does not prevent a 9.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. Optional: replace “Choose how strict.” with “Choose your limits.” It sounds less disciplinary and better supports the intended feeling that restraint should feel good.
2. Keep the customization note and leader. They explain something the overview alone would miss.

No essential visual correction is needed.

4. Settings

“Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.” is the best caption in the set. It is short, specific, and describes a meaningful product rule. The pending-change chip provides visible evidence, and the two leaders point to relevant controls.

The weakness is explanatory precision. “Raised from 4 to 5” makes the viewer reconstruct what happens now versus later. Meanwhile, the small pending-change chip contains the answer but will be hard to read when reduced. The dry-hours note explains timing without stating the actual overnight interval.

AI look 1: the sample state and targeted annotations feel deliberate. The density comes from the real settings page.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. Replace the first note with “5 drops starts at 4am. / Until then, you still get 4.” The existing chip already shows Undo.
2. Replace the second note with “Dry hours start tonight: / 11pm to 6am.”
3. Keep the headline. Do not add more annotations to explain the remaining controls.

These changes would make the frame understandable without reading the small chip and bring it to 9.

5. History

The chart is recognizable immediately. Blue bars connect it to the rest of the set, and the selected Saturday connects the graph to the daily details below. The arithmetic is coherent: 41 versus 48 minutes is approximately a 15% reduction.

The frame spends too much space explaining the comparison line. The subline mentions the previous week, the first annotation explains the dashed line, and the captured page includes its own legend. This repetition makes the image feel more like documentation than a store image.

AI look 1: the varied bars, selected date, and consistent figures look intentionally assembled. Nothing visibly suggests generated content.

Fixes, in impact order:

1. Remove “The dashed line is the week before.” and its leader. The captured legend already explains it.
2. Shorten the subline to “Sample week: 41 minutes a day.” Keep “sample” so the reduction does not read as a promised result.
3. Tighten the remaining note to “Pick a day to see / drops and cards used.”

That would bring it to 9 through subtraction. Keep the chart and daily cards intact.

The set

Story and order

Yes. Images 1 and 2 establish the essential loop: receive one video, watch within a limit, then stop or spend a saved drop. Keep them first.

Image 3 then explains that the limits are optional and adjustable. Settings explains the commitment mechanism, and History closes with feedback. The sequence works.

The weaker part is identifying YouTube at a glance. The first headline and both tiles could otherwise describe a separate video service. Naming YouTube in image 1’s headline would solve this.

Consistency

The repeated panel boundary, logo position, headline alignment, cream background, blue leaders, and restrained palette make the five screenshots feel like one set.

The right-hand pages have different visual densities. That is appropriate to their content. Do not force the lock, settings, and chart to use identical internal spacing.

Image 1 has the most conspicuous empty area between its explanation and bottom note. Image 2 uses that space more purposefully through the water. Images 4 and 5 have the greatest reading burden. Shorter annotations would improve balance more than extra decoration or more leaders.

Small-size legibility

The large serif headlines survive reduction. The product details do not survive equally well:

- Image 1 retains the single-video concept.
- Image 2 retains the lock and two choices.
- Image 3 retains three modes and a selected option.
- Image 4 becomes rows and switches; its caption must explain the delayed increase.
- Image 5 retains the chart, but loses most comparison and token labels.

The thin leaders and small endpoint dots become faint at thumbnail size. They should remain supporting navigation. None of the main claims should depend on seeing their endpoints.

C2 is the clearer small tile. C3’s yellow remains visible, but its meaning deteriorates sooner than its color.

Copy

No frame caption reads conspicuously as AI-written.

“Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.” is specific and worth keeping. “Your week in minutes.” is familiar but appropriate. “Choose how strict.” is the least appealing line because it makes the product sound punitive.

“Time for a breather.” is generic, but it belongs to the captured interface and does its job. The frame does not need another relaxation slogan around it.

The History frame’s repeated explanation of the prior week is the main copy problem. It is redundant rather than AI-like.

Rejection and misleading impressions

I see no obvious visual rejection trigger. The stated dimensions match Chrome’s screenshot and small-tile sizes. Caption panels and explanatory graphics are consistent with Chrome’s guidance allowing visual aids. [Chrome listing guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)

The main accuracy concern is keeping demonstration data distinct from results claims. Image 5 already says “sample week.” Preserve that qualification. Chrome requires screenshots and promotional images to represent the product accurately. [Listing requirements](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/listing-requirements/)

YouTube appears as a plain-text reference. There is no visible YouTube logo or endorsement claim. Keep drip’s branding dominant. The drawn thumbnail avoids presenting someone else’s thumbnail as listing artwork.

The lock’s blurred background reads as an intentional effect. The caption-panel water reads as explanatory artwork. Given the supplied explanation that the lock runs the real code, I would not flag either as a fabricated interface.
