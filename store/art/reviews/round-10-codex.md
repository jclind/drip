The set

The order works. Image 1 explains the replacement for the feed; image 2 explains the optional stopping point. Those are the right first two ideas. Modes, settings, and history then answer progressively more detailed questions. Keep this order.

The first image’s headline alone does not identify YouTube, so its subline does essential work. Keep that reference near the top. Image 2’s “if you set one” also matters: it prevents the lock from making daily limits seem mandatory.

Consistency is strong at the frame level. The divider, cream caption panel, logo position, headline alignment, and blue leaders establish one system. The right panels vary more in visual density: image 1 has one dominant object, image 2 has a large modal, images 3 and 4 become text-heavy, and image 5 returns to a strong graphic.

Do not force every capture to the same scale. Give each frame a clear subject at a readable size. Settings needs less material; the home page does not need more.

Empty space is generally useful. The larger problem is the long blue leaders crossing otherwise calm areas. Several terminate in gaps or beside labels rather than at the evidence. Removing redundant leaders would improve the set more than filling the blank space.

At roughly 320 pixels wide, the headlines, video, lock, selected mode outline, and chart remain recognizable. Most sublines, notes, and interface labels become unreadable. Images 3 and 4 suffer most because their differences depend on text. The thumbnails should communicate “one video,” “a stopping point,” “three choices,” “settings,” and “history,” without requiring the annotations.

No caption strongly reads as AI-written. The weakest line is “Customize picks which limits are on,” which sounds awkwardly constructed. “Time for a breather” is generic, but it is interface copy and outside this round’s permitted changes. “Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.” earns its balanced phrasing because it describes a specific behavior.
