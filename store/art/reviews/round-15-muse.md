The set

Does the order tell the story in the first one or two images? Yes, narrowly. Image 1 states the whole product and image 2 states the cost/consequence. That is the right opening pair. The drop is image 3: a settings-choice screen with the weakest headline arrives exactly where carousel attention falls off. Consider swapping 3 and 5 so proof of a calmer week comes third and configuration comes later.

Consistency across the five frames: Strong at the top - same mark, same serif headline size, same two-line sublines, same cream panel. It breaks lower down. Note vertical positions wander (mid-panel in 1 and 3, low in 5), leader lengths vary a lot, image 2 alone invades the caption panel with a dark band, and empty cream space ranges from modest in 4 to very large in 5. Capture scale improved in 1 and 4 this round but 3 and 5 now look slightly smaller by comparison. Set one note baseline position and one capture scale and apply to all five.

Legibility at small thumbnail size: Headlines survive. Everything else largely does not: sublines, notes, leader lines, the settings chip, mode card descriptions, and chart labels all disappear, leaving a cream block next to a dark block. That is acceptable for a carousel if the headline plus capture silhouette carry each frame, which is true for 1, 2, and 4 and not true for 3 (three grey slabs) or 5 (small blue bars). The tile fails its own test as detailed above.

Copy flagged as AI-written or generic: "Choose what drip does." is the worst - a headline that could sit on any product. "Each day shows drops and cards used." is passive template phrasing. "A drop is 15 minutes, by default." has the hedged comma of generated copy. The rest is good: "Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.", "When time runs out.", "Not this one? The next pick costs a card." all sound like a person with a product to sell.

Rejection or misleading risk: Low, with two watch items. Using the word YouTube in text with no YouTube logo or real thumbnails is nominative and should be fine; the drawn thumbnail is clearly drawn. Claims check out: 4 drops x 15m matches the stated 1h default, 41m vs 48m matches the 15% label, and screenshot 5 labels its data as sample. Residual risks are presentational rather than legal: screenshot 1's 34m next to 2 drops and screenshot 2's spare drop after a full day both look like the limit not working, and a reviewer or user could read that as the product not doing what the listing claims. Fix the arithmetic in sample data as noted above.

Ranked top-5 fixes across the set:
1. Redraw screenshot 1's thumbnail illustration with texture and asymmetry, and reposition screenshot 2's paused video so it is recognisable through the lock. This drives both worst AI-look scores and the 1-to-2 image connection.
2. Restore a benefit headline on screenshot 3 (reversing this round's headline change) and delete its second leader line.
3. Fix sample-data arithmetic: 30m not 34m on screenshot 1, and resolve the spare drop after 1h on screenshot 2.
4. Make the tile work at 110px: bigger tagline, smaller drop, "for YouTube" added, water band made clearly water or replaced with a keyline.
5. Normalise the frame system: one note position and capture scale across all five, finish or remove the half-cropped Finish video row on screenshot 4, and replace screenshot 5's spiked sample week with a flatter one.
