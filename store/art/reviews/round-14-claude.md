THE SET

Story in the first two: yes. 1 says what it replaces and how picking works. 2 shows the limit and the lock. A reader who stops at 2 knows the product. The search-only mode doesn't appear until 3. That's fine, since it's the least interesting mode.

Consistency: mark, headline and subline positions are identical across all five, and all headlines and sublines are two lines. Good. Right content left edges drift (620, 595, 597, 598, 605), with 1 the outlier. Right panel fill differs: 1, 3 and 4 are near black, 2 is a blurred scene, 5 is mid-gray. Empty space on the left is uneven. 4 and 5 have large empty lower halves, 1 is evenly spread, 2 is filled by the band.

Thumbnail legibility: the serif headlines hold at thumbnail size. Notes and leaders vanish, which is acceptable. 2's top half turns into a gray block at thumbnail size, so it looks like the weakest frame in the carousel strip. 1 and 4 read best small.

Copy flags:
- "Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen." is a parallel slogan, the most AI-shaped structure in the set, but it states a real rule. Keep it.
- "When time runs out." plus "Water rises as your watch time runs out." is a repeat; rewrite the band note.
- "The default: 4 drops..." has a colon connector, minor.
- Frame 5's subline repeats panel numbers.
- "A Chrome extension for YouTube that..." is generic but useful for search, so keep it.
- Nothing else reads as filler.

Rejection or misleading risk: low. YouTube appears only as text (nominative use). There's no YouTube logo, the play icon on Watch isn't YouTube's mark, and the thumbnail and channel are invented. The token math is consistent across frames 1 to 5 and with the default in 3. The history week is labeled sample data. The one soft spot is frame 5's subline presenting a decrease as the headline-level message. It's labeled, but a subline about what the page shows removes the question.
