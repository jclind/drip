IMAGE 1 (home, one revealed video)

To reach 9 and 2: close the dead zone in the left panel and give the thumbnail illustration one detail that's clearly hand-chosen, so it stops reading as generic flat-vector stock.

Pushes up: the headline names the product's promise in five words. The token legend sits right under the subline, so the drop and card are defined before the right panel uses them. The top bar (34m left, 2 drops, 7 cards) matches the legend's icons exactly. The leader lands right on the Reveal next button, which carries the "-1 card" cost.

Pushes down: the left panel has about 280px of empty cream between the legend (ends near y 390) and the note (y 672), so the caption half looks bottom-heavy and unfinished. The cyclist-on-a-ridge illustration with layered hills and a gradient sky is the kind of image an image generator or stock vector pack produces by default. It's the main AI-look driver here, even though the owner chose to keep it. The elbowed leader also runs a long way across empty space before it turns down.

What works: the sample numbers agree with each other. The right panel fills the full height with no dead band. The headline type has real character.

Fixes, by impact:
1. Left panel gap. Move the token legend down so it sits roughly midway between the subline and the note, or move the note up to y ~600 and keep the elbow short. Either way, no single gap should be over ~150px.
2. Thumbnail. Add one specific, slightly odd detail (a rider's jacket color picked from drip's blue, a sign post, a puddle) so it reads as drawn for drip and not as a default landscape. Keep the composition.
3. Headline leads with "YouTube" in the largest type on the page. It's descriptive use and probably fine, but "Your YouTube home, one video at a time." or a smaller "for YouTube" in the subline lowers the trademark exposure in the image that matters most.

THE SET

Story in the first two: yes. Image 1 says what drip does and defines both tokens. Image 2 shows the consequence. A shopper who sees only two images gets the product. Image 3 is the weakest frame and sits third, which is fine but means the set dips right after the strongest pair.

Consistency: the left panels are consistent (logo, headline position, two-line sublines, note type size). The right panels are not. 1, 2 and 5 fill the height. 3 and 4 have 140 to 160px of dead black at the top. Leader styles mix straight (2, 3, 4) and elbowed (1, 5), which is fine since each targets a real element, but the long elbows in 1 and 5 look busier. Left-panel empty space is uneven, with big gaps in 1, 4 and 5.

Small thumbnails: headlines read in all five. The notes vanish, which is acceptable. Right panels 1, 2 and 5 read as shapes (video card, lock card, bar chart). 3 and 4 become grey lists. The tile works at small size, c3 better.

Copy flags:
- Screenshot 4 note 2, "no watching on drops", is clumsy.
- Screenshot 4 repeats one fact three times.
- Screenshot 3 note 1 restates the card.
- "Your week in minutes." is generic.
- Screenshot 2 says "pauses" while the product says "stops".
None of the lines read as obviously AI-written. The headlines are short and specific enough.

Rejection or misleading risk: low. No YouTube logo, no real thumbnails. The right panels look like YouTube's dark UI because drip runs on YouTube's page, which is accurate. "YouTube" as the first word of image 1's headline is the only trademark exposure, and it's descriptive use. Screenshot 2's note about rising water describes motion the still doesn't show. That's not misleading, but fix 1 below resolves it anyway. Screenshot 5's sample data is labeled.

Top 5 fixes across the set:
1. Screenshot 2: show a visible water surface on the right and line the left band's crest up with it at the seam, same y and same color. Or remove the band.
2. Screenshot 4: cut the 4-to-5 repeat to one place, and rewrite note 2 as "Add dry hours and they start tonight." so both halves of the headline get a note.
3. Screenshots 3 and 4: rescale or recenter the captured page so the right panel has no 140px dead band at the top, matching 1, 2 and 5.
4. Screenshot 3: replace note 1 with a line the card doesn't already say, ideally one that names drops and cards.
5. Pick tile c3, and rotate or offset the card so it reads as a card at small size.
