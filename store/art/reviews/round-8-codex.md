The strongest introduction. One video dominates the page, and the headline states the central promise. Cream, black, blue, and restrained gold give it a clear identity.

What lowers the score: the frame explains recommendations and cards but leaves the prominent watch-time display unexplained. The first leader points at an unmarked edge of the thumbnail. The large gap between the two notes makes the explanation feel disconnected.

Fixes, in impact order:
- Change the lower note to “Stash it, or pass.\nOne card reveals the next video.” This connects the token to the action more directly.
- Move that note slightly upward and angle its leader toward “Reveal next.” The current endpoint sits beside Stash, while the second sentence explains revealing.
- Shorten the subline to “Your YouTube recommendations.\nOne video at a time.” This removes the awkward “one video from it.”

Those frame changes would bring it toward 9. Keep the illustration. Its broad landscape shapes have a slight stock illustration feel, which accounts for AI look 2, but nothing visibly looks broken or generated.

The set

The first two images tell the core story: reveal one recommendation, then pause at an optional limit. But they introduce enforcement before showing the range of modes. I would use 1, 3, 2, 4, 5. That establishes choice before explaining the lock.

The five frames have a strong shared structure. The left margin, small mark, serif headlines, panel split, and blue leaders are consistent. Two-line headlines generally behave well. Screenshot 4 has the longest lines and least spare width. Screenshot 2 has three callouts, while the others have two. History fills the panel most aggressively; Settings contains the most competing detail. These differences need selective cropping, not identical page scaling.

At thumbnail size, the headlines, video, lock, selected mode outline, and chart survive. Most sublines and callouts will disappear. Settings suffers most because its distinguishing feature depends on reading small labels. The thin leaders become faint traces. They should support full-size viewing rather than carry essential meaning.

No caption strongly reads as AI-written. “Pick your mode” and “See where the time went” are familiar product phrases, but both describe the content. “Your week, one bar per day” repeats what the chart already shows. “Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.” earns its place through a specific product rule.

Rules and misleading impressions

The stated dimensions match Chrome’s screenshot and small promo formats. Captioned frames and visual explanations fit its guidance. The main concern is text density, especially Settings. Chrome also asks that images accurately represent the current experience. [Chrome listing image guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)

I see no obvious YouTube endorsement claim, copied thumbnail, or misleading Google badge. The plain YouTube references explain compatibility. The illustrated video could briefly suggest drip supplies its own curated catalog; “Your YouTube recommendations” in image 1 would reduce that ambiguity.

The lock’s intentional background blur represents the described interface behavior. I would not flag it as a blurry screenshot defect. The staged water and sample state are reasonable demonstrations given your explanation.

The clearest claim to qualify is “A drop is 15 minutes,” since the settings visibly make that configurable. Also, “3 times a week” obscures the rolling window.
