Across the set

The order mostly tells a story: replace the feed, stop when time runs out, choose a mode, configure it, review usage. The first image establishes the main benefit, provided visitors read the subline. The second establishes a stopping mechanism, but currently makes that mechanism seem compulsory.

Keep the order if screenshot 2 explicitly says limits are optional. Otherwise, move mode choice ahead of the lock.

The outer frames are consistent. Panel position, corner radius, left margin, footer mark, colors, and typography hold together. The caption groups also appear vertically centered consistently; their different starting heights follow from different line counts.

The inner captures need more consistency:
- Screenshot 1 has generous surrounding space and relatively small content.
- Screenshot 2 isolates one large dialog.
- Screenshot 3 fills its panel with a complete page.
- Screenshot 4 fills the height and cuts into another section.
- Screenshot 5 starts almost flush at the top and ends mid-section.

Use a consistent principle: each frame should show one complete, readable feature area. Identical scale across different pages matters less than comparable visual weight and deliberate crop boundaries.

At small sizes, the captions and blue accents survive. Most product text does not. At half size, a roughly 14-pixel interface label becomes roughly 7 pixels. Smaller carousel thumbnails turn the settings and history details into texture. Screenshot 1 particularly needs a larger product view. Tile c survives reduction best; tile b’s tagline is the first element to disappear.

The copy is mostly specific. There are no obvious AI-written lines. The weaker lines have other problems:
- “Pick how much drip does.” is vague.
- “A search box alone” sounds slightly unnatural.
- “When the glass runs dry” has character, but asks viewers to decode a metaphor.
- “Make every drop count.” is the most stock marketing phrase, though it fits the brand.
- “A late night can’t talk you out of it” feels authored and human. Its drawback is length.

Listing and branding risks

- The supplied dimensions match Google’s screenshot and small promo formats. Five screenshots also fit the allowed maximum. Google asks for square outer corners and full-bleed images. These attachments visibly have full rectangular backgrounds; rounded panels inside the artwork are a separate design choice. [Image requirements](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/images)
- The main accuracy risks are the known draft lock state and any final capture that differs from the shipped behavior. Google requires accurate listing metadata and current screenshots. The absolute “video stops” claim also needs care because Settings shows a “Finish video when time runs out” option. [Listing policy](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/listing-requirements), [Screenshot guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)
- I see no obvious YouTube impersonation in these images. The drip identity is distinct. Keep YouTube references descriptive, follow Google’s compatibility and attribution guidance in the listing, and avoid implying endorsement. [Branding guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/branding)
- The drawn thumbnail avoids using someone else’s thumbnail. There is no blanket ban on real YouTube thumbnails in the cited Chrome policy; permission and intellectual property rights are the issue. Keeping the illustration is reasonable. [Intellectual property policy](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/impersonation-and-intellectual-property)
