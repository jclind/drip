Image 1 — One video at a time

What pushes the score up or down:
Up: the headline states the whole product in four words and the right strip proves it with a single large card. The Watch / Stash / Reveal next hierarchy is readable at full size. The leader to "Reveal next · -1" lands exactly on the button.
Down: three numbers compete in the top bar — 34m left, 2 drops, 7 cards — and their relationship is not visible. 2 drops reads as 30 minutes, not 34, and 7 cards contradicts the "10 a day" default introduced two images later. A viewer will do that maths and stall. The bottom-left quadrant of the caption panel is empty beige for roughly a third of the frame. The legend icons sit lower than the thumbnail's vertical centre, so the eye drops into the gap between the subline and the legend.

What works:
Headline, subline and single-card proof are aligned. The thumbnail is big enough to read as a video, not a feed.

What to fix, by impact:
1. Sample data: make the numbers self-evidently consistent. Show "60m left today" beside 4 drops, or keep 34m but change the caption note to state the split, e.g. "34m left of today's hour. Drops are banked for later." As drawn, the frame asks the viewer to trust an invisible rule.
2. Thumbnail illustration: the flat storm cloud, gradient dusk sky, layered ridge silhouettes and pea-sized cyclist are the single most AI-looking element in the set. Redraw with specific, observed detail — a rutted trail, puddles, a rider with weight on the pedals, rain only under the cloud's trailing edge — and give the hills a broken, non-repeating contour. That is what takes AI look from 4 to 2.
3. Frame: close the beige void. Move the legend block up to sit under the subline as one unit, and let the "Not this one?" note rise with it; leave the empty space at the very bottom instead of in the middle of the reading path. This reverses the kept decision on legend position — I am reversing it because the gap reads as an unfinished layout at thumbnail size, not as breathing room.
4. Legend icons: the drop and card carry a sticker-like offset shadow that the header icons do not. Use the same flat rendering as the top bar so the tokens look like one drawn set, not pasted emoji.
5. Caption copy: "A drop is 15 minutes by default" is correct but flat. "One drop buys 15 minutes" says the same thing and teaches the verb the lock screen later uses.

To reach 9: items 1–3. To reach AI look 2: item 2, plus item 4.

Versus previous: Better — "by default" without the comma reads cleanly, and the lighter near hills and brighter trail separate the rider from the ridge.

Image 2 — When time runs out

What pushes the score up or down:
Up: the lock panel is centred, high contrast, and the +15m / Done for now pair is the clearest two-button choice in the set. The blurred, tinted background does read as "something paused behind this."
Down: the background is a blue-to-green gradient wash with no recognisable video in it. Because the drawn video is positioned so little shows, the frame cannot demonstrate the note's claim about water over a paused video — it demonstrates a gradient. The three bubbles, hand-placed and hand-sized, sit in open water with no source and even spacing; that is the most obviously staged element in the set and it drives the AI look score. The caption panel's dark water band is a different colour and a different water from the green wash on the right, so the two halves argue with each other. "I need this one · 3 left this week" is mid-grey on black and disappears at thumbnail size.

What works:
The panel itself. Title, watched-today line, two actions, one escape hatch — the hierarchy is right.

What to fix, by impact:
1. Sample state / crop: shift the paused video so a recognisable piece of it — the ridge line and trail — survives the blur in the top third of the strip, above the panel. Without that, the central promise of this image is asserted in the caption and absent in the picture.
2. Illustration: remove two bubbles, keep one small one low and off-axis, and soften it further or drop bubbles entirely. Hand-placed decorative bubbles are what make a stopped animation look generated rather than frozen.
3. Frame: match the caption panel's water band to the lock's green-blue, or remove the band. Two different waters in one frame reads as two assets composited.
4. Scale: enlarge the lock panel's strip slightly so "I need this one" survives at thumbnail size. That text is the difference between a limit and a trap, and it is currently the least legible line on the right.
5. Caption copy: "At zero, the water rises over the paused video" describes motion the still cannot show. "At zero, the video pauses under water" describes the state actually pictured.

To reach 9: items 1, 3 and 4. To reach AI look 2: item 2, plus removing the generic gradient feel in item 1 by letting real video shapes show through.

Versus previous: Better — three smaller bubbles with softer rims are less decorative, and less dark patch shows at the top left.

Image 3 — Choose your limits

What pushes the score up or down:
Up: three options, one pre-selected, one marked Recommended, a Next button. This is the only frame where the right strip explains itself without the caption. The blue selection outline matches the leader lines.
Down: the three miniature previews on the right of each card are abstract grey and yellow bars in black boxes. They look like placeholder wireframes, not like Quiet, Curated and Full drip, and at thumbnail size they are noise. The headline now promises "limits," but Quiet is not a limit, it is a removal — the subline ("Search only, one pick at a time, or daily watch limits") covers it, the headline does not. "Customize" is doing important work and is styled as a minor link with a leader dot smaller than the radio buttons.

What works:
The default note is concrete — "4 drops and 10 cards a day" — and it matches "1h of watching and 10 new videos a day" on the card. Numbers agreeing across the frame is what images 1 and 2 fail to do.

What to fix, by impact:
1. Illustration: replace the three abstract mini-previews with literal miniatures — an empty search box, a single card, a card plus a timer bar. Abstract bars are the main AI/template signal here.
2. Caption copy: headline and subline repeat each other in structure. Shorten the subline to what the headline cannot say: "You can change this later. For the first 24 hours, changes apply at once."
3. Frame: give "Customize" the same visual weight as the notes pointing at it. It currently looks like a footnote that two leader lines are trying to promote.
4. Sample data: the selected card says the video "stops when the time runs out," which image 2 shows as a choice, not a stop. Align the caption note, not the product text: "The default: 4 drops and 10 cards a day. Time out means a choice, not a cut-off" is too long — instead change the left note to "The default: 4 drops, 10 cards, and a pause when time runs out."

To reach 9: items 1 and 2. To reach AI look 2: item 1 — real miniatures instead of generated-looking bars.

Versus previous: Better — "Choose your limits." removes the duplication with the page's own "How much should drip do?" and promises something the cards then deliver.

Image 4 — Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen

What pushes the score up or down:
Up: the headline is the best line in the set. It names a real, unusual product behaviour and the frame shows it happening — raised to 5, effective 4 until 4:00am, with Undo.
Down: this is the densest strip and the least legible at small size. The "4 drops until 4:00am · Undo" pill, both stepper readouts, the segmented Mode control and both time dropdowns are all below thumbnail legibility. The last row, "Finish video when time runs out," is cut by the bottom edge with its toggle half in frame, which reads as a crop error rather than a continuation. The left panel has two notes spread over a tall empty field, so the leaders are long and the eye travels far for small facts.

What works:
Showing a pending loosening, with Undo, is evidence rather than a claim. The headline earns its keep.

What to fix, by impact:
1. Crop: shift the captured page up so the final toggle sits fully inside the frame with margin, even if that costs part of the Settings / Channels / History tab row. A half-clipped control is the first thing a reviewer reads as carelessness.
2. Scale: this strip needs the largest type in the set and has the smallest. Narrow the caption panel for this frame so the settings strip gains width; the two short notes do not need 44% of the image.
3. Caption copy: "Raised to 5 drops. The 5th waits for the reset." is good. Add the reset time to the note — "until 4:00am" — so the reader does not have to find 7-point text in the pill to complete the sentence.
4. Sample data: the two notes describe a loosening and a schedule. The strip also shows Minutes per drop at 15m, which no note touches; either point a short note at it or accept it as set dressing. Currently it is a third unexplained control competing with the two explained ones.

To reach 9: items 1 and 2. To reach AI look 2: this frame is already close — it looks like a real product screen. Removing the clipped row and matching the caption panel's generous spacing to the strip's density gets it there.

Versus previous: Better — "Dry hours: 11pm to 6am, starting tonight" gives the actual schedule the strip shows, instead of only announcing that dry hours exist.

Image 5 — Your week in minutes

What pushes the score up or down:
Up: one number, one chart, one comparison line, and an explicit "Sample week" disclosure in the caption. The Friday spike gives the chart a shape that is not a decorative ramp.
Down: the chart's y-axis is labelled only "2h" and "0", the dashed average line is explained below the chart in small type, and the day letters use T twice with no way to tell Tuesday from Thursday except position. The three tiles at the bottom describe one day, Saturday, while the chart describes seven — the frame's two halves are about different units and the note has to stitch them together. Nothing marks Saturday's bar as the selected one except a slightly darker column behind it and an underlined S, both easy to miss.

What works:
Stating that the week is sample data, in the caption, is the right call and protects the whole set.

What to fix, by impact:
1. Sample data: pick a selected day whose numbers tell a story against the chart. Saturday at 30m with 2 drops used is 30 minutes from 2 drops — that is exactly 15 minutes each, which quietly teaches the token. Say so in the note: "Saturday: 30m, exactly 2 drops." That makes the tiles evidence instead of three more numbers.
2. Crop/scale: enlarge the chart area relative to the tiles. The chart is the image's claim; the tiles are footnotes and currently take a third of the strip.
3. Caption copy: "Each day shows its drops, cards, and unlocks" is a description of the interface, not a reason to care. "Tap a day to see what it cost" or the item-1 version gives the reader an action or a fact.
4. Illustration/chart rendering: add a visible selection state to Saturday's bar in the captured state — a brighter outline or a marker above the bar — so the connection between chart and tiles does not depend on one underlined letter.

To reach 9: items 1 and 2. To reach AI look 2: item 1. Real, slightly irregular sample numbers that reconcile with each other are the strongest human signal available here; a generated week would not add up.
