The set

The first two images tell the core story: replace the feed with one pick, then stop when the chosen time runs out. Keep that order. Search-only mode appears later, which is acceptable because image 1 establishes the distinguishing experience first.

The five frames share a clear system: cream caption panel, consistent split, serif headline, small mark, blue leaders, and dark product capture. Different interface scales make sense for the different subjects. Settings is the density outlier. History has the largest uninterrupted empty caption area, but its bottom annotation earns that space.

At thumbnail size, the headlines and broad interface shapes survive. The notes, metadata, pending-change badge, and mode descriptions do not. Image 4 loses the most meaning. Image 1 still communicates “one video,” and image 2 still communicates “stop.” The tile has the strongest small-size recognition.

No frame copy reads conspicuously AI-written. “Choose your limits” is generic and less precise than the alternatives shown. “Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.” has a polished advertising cadence, but the captured pending setting substantiates it. “Time for a breather” is familiar interface language, not a reason to change the frame.

I see no obvious visual rejection trigger. The supplied dimensions match Chrome’s published screenshot and small-tile sizes.
