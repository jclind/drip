Across the set

The first two images tell the story: one recommendation replaces the feed, and an optional limit can stop playback. Image 3 then explains the available modes. The current order works.

The consistent divider, cream caption panel, serif headlines, small mark, and blue leaders make the five screenshots belong together. Keeping each real page’s background also avoids making the captures feel pasted into arbitrary mockups.

The main inconsistency is information density. Images 1 and 2 have one obvious subject. Images 3 and 4 require more reading. Image 5 regains a strong visual subject through the chart. Settings needs the most editorial cropping.

At thumbnail size, the headlines and major shapes survive. Most sublines, token definitions, settings labels, and leader endpoints will not. Image 4 suffers most because its subject becomes a collection of small controls. Thin leaders should remain supporting aids; they cannot carry the thumbnail story.

No caption strongly reads as AI-written. “Or set your own mix.” is the most generic. “Time for a breather.” is familiar language, but it fits the lock’s purpose and belongs to the product interface. The strongest copy is “One video at a time.” and “Easy to tighten. Slow to loosen.”

No earlier decision needs reversing.

Store rules and misleading impressions

The supplied dimensions match Google’s screenshot and small promo tile sizes. Annotated screenshots are compatible with its guidance to use visual aids. Google also advises limiting text and keeping screenshots representative of the actual experience. [Chrome listing image guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/best-listing)

I see no obvious rejection trigger in the visible artwork. YouTube appears as descriptive text; no YouTube logo or endorsement claim is visible. The intentional lock blur communicates a product effect.

The main accuracy concerns are the unqualified 15-minute definition and the small sample-data disclosure. Google’s listing rules require screenshots and promotional images to be accurate.
