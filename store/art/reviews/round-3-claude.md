1. screenshot-1 (home page, one revealed video)

Score drivers. This is the strongest frame. In one glance you get the product: a stat row (34m left today, 2 drops, 7 cards), one video, and a "Reveal next -1 card" action. The drawn thumbnail works. It has a clear subject and a mood, and it doesn't pretend to be a real YouTube video. What holds the score down is the bottom crop. The Stashed / Reveal next / X row sits about 30px off the canvas edge, which looks cut off rather than bled. There's also a lot of dead navy in the left column above and below the caption.

What works. The headline "One video at a time." is short, concrete, and true. The token icons show up here first, so later frames can lean on them. The sample data hangs together: 34m left with 2 drops in stock, and 7 cards.

Fixes, by impact:
- Scale the captured page down about 5 to 8 percent, or shift it up, so the button row gets 50 to 60px of clear space below it. Right now the most important action, Reveal next, looks cropped.
- The subline repeats the headline ("one video" twice). Change it to something like "drip swaps YouTube's home feed for a single pick. Spend a card to see the next." That adds the card mechanic without the echo.
- Optional: nudge the caption block up about 20px so it lines up with the thumbnail's vertical center instead of the canvas center. The left column would look less like it's floating.

2. screenshot-2 (lock screen)

Score drivers. The lock card is clean and easy to read. The frame around it is weak. The brief promises "blue water rising" and a paused video under the lock. At this size I can't see either. The background reads as a dark navy smudge with a faint glow at the left edge. The bubbles are spread evenly over the whole panel, including the top third, so they look like dust or specks on the screen, not bubbles in water. Because the panel background is almost the same color as the frame, the panel edge nearly disappears, and this frame loses the panel structure the other four have.

What works. The headline "Out of minutes? The lock comes up." is direct. The data matches the default (1h watched = 4 x 15m). "I need this one · 3 left this week" lines up with the Settings value of 3 in screenshot 4.

Fixes, by impact:
- Pick a moment with the water visibly higher, a clear lighter-blue band covering the lower third to half of the panel with a visible waterline. Keep the bubbles inside the water, rising from below the card. Right now the product's signature visual doesn't come through.
- Make the paused video readable under the lock. Brighten the dimmed thumbnail, or let the illustration's ridge and sky show at the top of the panel above the water. The viewer should see that the lock covers a video, not an empty page.
- Give the panel the same visible edge or tone shift as the other frames. A slightly lighter panel base, or the 1px border at the same contrast as screenshots 1, 3, and 4, would do it.
- The subline mixes two ideas: "Limits are optional" and the drop/quit choice. Try "Spend a drop for 15 more minutes, or call it a day." and let screenshot 3 carry "optional".
- Possible viewer confusion: the default day is 4 drops and all 4 are used, yet the lock offers "+15m · -1 drop". A careful reader asks where the fifth drop comes from. If drops carry over or refill, the sample state could show that, or the subline could say so. If you can't explain it in the frame, choose a sample state where it's obvious, like "45m watched today".

3. screenshot-3 (welcome page, mode choice)

Score drivers. The panel is well filled, and the three options give a quick read of the product's range. The selected "Full drip" card with the Recommended pill is a nice focal point. The caption and the panel ask the same question back to back: "How strict should it be?" next to "How much should drip do?" That's the main drag on the score. The mini diagrams on the right of each option are too small and abstract to read even at full size, and at thumbnail size they're gray blobs.

What works. The subline maps exactly onto the three options in order (hide feeds / one at a time / daily limit), so the frame teaches the modes without extra text. The panel crop is the most balanced of the five: content runs from y=125 to y=715 with even margins.

Fixes, by impact:
- Change the caption so it doesn't repeat the panel heading. Make it a statement, for example "Pick how strict it is." or "Three levels. Change anytime." The second also uses the "change it in Settings" line instead of making the reader find it in small gray text.
- Scale the captured page up about 5 percent so the option descriptions read better as carousel thumbnails. There's room on the right, since the panel content stops at about x=1233 with the bleed beyond.
- The mini diagrams are interface, so they stay as they are. A slightly larger scale helps them too.

4. screenshot-4 (Settings tab)

Score drivers. It's honest and complete, but it's the densest frame and the least visual: nine rows of controls, all the same weight. The caption leans on drip's own vocabulary ("drops", "dry hours"), which a first-time store visitor hasn't learned yet. The subline's "stop paid watching overnight" is the riskiest phrase in the set. "Paid" will read to many people as a paid subscription feature, not "watching you pay for with drops". There's also an empty strip under the last divider.

What works. Real numbers that agree with the other frames (4 drops, 15m, 3 "I need this one"). The Mode segmented control at the top ties back to screenshot 3. Toggles and steppers are clear.

Fixes, by impact:
- Rewrite the subline without "paid". For example: "A drop is 15 minutes of watching. Dry hours turn YouTube off overnight." Check that this matches the behavior. If dry hours only stop drop-funded watching, say "Dry hours pause your drops overnight."
- Change the headline to plain language. "Set your daily limit." or "Your limit, your hours." Teach "drops" in the subline, which already does.
- Crop tighter on the Watch time section and scale up about 10 percent. Fewer, larger rows read better in the carousel, and the dead space under the last divider goes away. If anything has to go off the bottom, it should be "I need this one".

5. screenshot-5 (History tab)

Score drivers. The chart is the most immediately readable visual in the set, and the sample data holds up under a check. The bars average to about 41m, the dashed line sits at 48m, the 15% drop is right, and Saturday's 27m matches the bar. The negative is structural. The History content sits in its own lighter rounded card inside the black panel, so this frame shows a card within a panel within a frame. None of the other four does that, and it reads as a different template. The chart also uses only the bottom half of its 2h scale, which leaves a large empty band at the top of the plot.

What works. The headline "See your week." is short. The three stat tiles (drops, cards, unlocks) are the one place the set shows all three token types together. "Of 7 cards, 1 saved, 2 watched, and 4 passed." is a nice specific detail.

Fixes, by impact:
- Crop and scale so the History card fills the panel the way content does in screenshots 1, 3, and 4. Scale up about 10 to 12 percent and center it, so the black margin around the inner card shrinks to a thin edge or bleeds out. The double box goes away, and the chart gets bigger.
- Raise the sample data so the tallest day comes near 1h30 to 1h45. The bars then fill the plot and the week reads as a real pattern. Keep the averages and the 15% consistent.
- The subline says "Compare each day's watch time with the week before." The chart compares each day with last week's average line. "Watch time by day, against last week's average." is accurate. If that's dull, "Know whether this week was better." puts the benefit first.

6. tile-c (wordmark only)

Score drivers. Clean, balanced, and readable at small size. But it's a flat drop and a bold sans wordmark on dark navy, the default look of a thousand generated logos, and it says nothing about what the extension does. In store search, next to tiles with a product cue, it carries no information.

What works. The drop's single highlight stroke gives it a little character. Good contrast. The mark and the word are well matched in weight.

Fixes, by impact:
- Use tile-d2 instead, or add a product cue (a hint of the single-video card or the gold card token).
- If you keep it, make the drop slightly smaller relative to the wordmark. The x-height of "drip" should be the thing that reads in a 220px-wide list view.

7. tile-d2 (wordmark plus tagline)

Score drivers. Same mark, plus a line that tells you what it is. The tagline is left-aligned to the wordmark's stem, which is a deliberate touch. It repeats screenshot 1's headline, which is good for recall. The tagline is small relative to the tile. At the store's small list size it will be near the edge of legibility.

What works. The three-line hierarchy (mark, name, promise) is the right amount of information for a promo tile. "One video at a time." is specific to this product.

Fixes, by impact:
- Increase the tagline size about 15 to 20 percent and tighten the gap above it, keeping the left alignment. It needs to survive at half scale.
- The group sits slightly left of center and slightly high. Re-center it optically on the combined bounding box (drop + text block).
- Optional: tint the tagline a cooler gray-blue so it reads as secondary without losing contrast.

The set

Story in the first one or two images. Yes. Screenshot 1 shows the core idea (one video, a card to reveal the next, minutes left), and screenshot 2 shows the consequence (the lock). A shopper who stops after two images knows what drip does. Screenshot 2 is visually the weakest of the five, though, which hurts more in second place than it would later.

Consistency. The frame itself is consistent: panel top-left at the same spot, bleed off right and bottom, captions vertically centered, drip mark bottom-left. Caption lengths are close (one- or two-line headline, two-line subline every time). The breaks are inside the panels. Screenshot 2's panel nearly disappears into the background. Screenshot 5 adds an inner card. Screenshots 1 and 4 crop tight at the bottom while 3 and 5 have room. Panel fill varies from dense (4) to sparse (2). The left column has about 250px of empty navy above and below each caption in every frame. That's acceptable restraint, but it's identical in all five, which adds to the template feel.

Legibility at small thumbnail size. All headlines read. Panel content: screenshot 1 (video and stat row) and screenshot 5 (bars) read. Screenshot 2's card reads, but the water doesn't. Screenshots 3 and 4 turn into gray lists. Scaling up 3, 4, and 5 helps most.

Copy that reads as AI-written or generic:
- "See your week." is generic. It could be any tracker app.
- "Choose your drops and dry hours." is jargon to a new visitor. It reads as invented vocabulary dropped in for flavor.
- "stop paid watching overnight" reads oddly and suggests a paid tier.
- "How strict should it be?" isn't AI-sounding, but it duplicates the panel heading, which looks careless.
- "One video at a time." and "Out of minutes? The lock comes up." read as human and specific.

Rejection or misleading risks:
- "YouTube" in captions is descriptive use and normally fine. No YouTube logo or red play button appears. Keep it that way, including in the tiles, which currently don't use the name. Good.
- Screenshot 1 imitates YouTube's card layout with a fictional channel and view count. That's acceptable because it's drip's real UI, but don't add a real channel name or a real thumbnail.
- "paid watching" in screenshot 4 could be read as a claim that there's a paid product. Remove it.
- Screenshot 2's lock offering a drop after a full default day could look like a contradiction to a reviewer comparing frames. Low risk, but cheap to fix.
- The welcome copy "1h of watching and 10 new videos a day" agrees with the defaults shown elsewhere (4 x 15m). No mismatch found.

Top 5 fixes across the set:
1. Screenshot 2: show the water. Use a later moment with a visible waterline, bubbles only below it, and the paused video readable above or through it, plus a visible panel edge. The lock is the second image and drip's most distinctive visual, and right now it reads as an empty dark box.
2. Screenshot 4: rewrite the subline to drop "paid watching", and change the headline from "drops and dry hours" to plain words. Then crop tighter on the Watch time block and scale up.
3. Screenshot 5: scale the History card up to fill the panel so it stops looking like a card in a panel, and raise the sample data so the bars fill the chart.
4. Screenshot 3: change the caption so it no longer repeats "How much should drip do?" (for example "Pick how strict it is.") and scale up about 5 percent.
5. Use tile-d2 over tile-c, with the tagline enlarged about 15 to 20 percent and the group re-centered optically.
